# Why we're investing in developer relations

We're hiring a developer relations team. Here's the reasoning behind it.

Most of our new customers over the past year came from someone reading our docs, trying our SDK, or watching a conference talk before they ever spoke to sales. That path is now the default, not the exception. If a developer can't figure out how our API works from the documentation alone, we lose them before a salesperson gets a chance to help.

Support tickets tell the same story. A large share of the questions we get aren't about bugs. They're about missing examples, unclear error messages, and gaps between what the docs describe and what the SDK actually does. Those are DevRel problems, and until now nobody owned them full time.

The plan is direct: better documentation, real example code instead of stub snippets, and engineers who spend time in our Discord and GitHub issues answering questions as they come in. We also want people who can sit in on customer calls and bring what they hear back to the product team, since a lot of our best fixes have started as an offhand comment from a developer stuck at 11pm.

This isn't a marketing hire. The team reports through engineering, and success looks like fewer support tickets, faster time from signup to first working request, and docs that developers trust enough to stop asking us questions we already answered.

If you've done this kind of work before, we're hiring.
