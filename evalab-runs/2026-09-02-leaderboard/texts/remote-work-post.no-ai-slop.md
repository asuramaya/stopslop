Our office lease ended last March and we didn't renew. Twenty-two people, three time zones, no building.

The first thing to go was the calendar. We killed daily standup in week two and replaced it with a written check-in nobody has to read live. Meeting time per person dropped from about fourteen hours a week to five. I expected people to feel disconnected. Mostly they slept in.

The bigger shift was that writing became the job. A decision that used to happen at someone's desk now needs a doc with the options and the tradeoff written down. It's slower to start and much cheaper to revisit. When we hired two backend engineers in December, they read the decision log for a day and shipped to production in their first week. That never happened in the office.

Two things hurt. Salary bands got ugly fast once we hired outside our metro, and we ended up publishing one band per role regardless of city, which costs us roughly 8% more per hire. And our two most junior engineers stalled. They didn't know what to ask or who to ask it of, and Slack gave them nowhere to overhear the answer. We put them on two hours of scheduled pairing a day starting in month five, and both are fine now.

We fly everyone in twice a year for three days: planning, one long dinner, no talks. That runs about $40,000 a year against the $210,000 the office cost us.

If we ran the year again, the pairing would start in week one.
