## How we work

We're small enough that you'll know what everyone is building, and why.

Engineers here own things end to end. The design sketch, the code, the deploy, the 3 a.m. page. There's no ops team to hand it to. That sounds heavier than it is. Most weeks nobody gets paged, and whoever does takes the next day off. Code review is required, and it's fast. Median time to first comment is under two hours. If your PR sits for a day, that's our failure, not yours. We argue in the review thread and settle it there, and nobody gets to reopen a merged decision in a meeting three weeks later.

Two recurring meetings. A 30-minute Monday sync, and a Thursday demo where you show something running, not slides about something you plan to run. Everything else is written down, because written arguments survive people leaving and hallway conversations don't.

You will ship in your first week.

Probably something small and slightly terrifying. We'd rather you learn the deploy pipeline on day three with a one-line change than on day sixty with a migration. We've decided against stack ranking, against on-call rotations with fewer than five people, and against any version of "we'll fix the tests later." Roughly a fifth of every quarter goes to whatever the team says is rotting, and engineers pick that list, not managers. If you want to read the code before you decide, ask in your first interview. We'll send it.
