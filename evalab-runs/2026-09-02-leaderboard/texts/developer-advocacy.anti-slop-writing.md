We hired a developer advocate last month. Here's the reasoning, since a few people asked.

Our docs were fine. Not great, fine. The quickstart worked if you already knew what you were doing, and about 40% of trial signups never got a successful API call in week one. We saw the number in March, argued about it for a while, and mostly blamed onboarding UX. Then Priya sat in on four support calls and came back with something less comfortable: people weren't confused by the product. They were confused about whether the product was for them at all.

That's not a docs problem. You can't fix it with a better error message either.

What fixes it, as far as I can tell, is somebody who talks to developers in public, regularly, without a pitch attached. Conference talks, sure, but mostly the boring stuff. Answering questions in our Discord at 11pm. Writing the ugly integration guide nobody on the product team wants to write because it involves three legacy auth flows. Filing bugs against our own SDK after using it like a customer would.

I'll admit I was skeptical. DevRel budgets get cut first in every downturn I've lived through, and the ROI story is genuinely hard to make. Attribution is a mess. Someone reads a blog post in January, signs up in September, and no dashboard will ever connect those.

So we're not measuring it that way. We're measuring time-to-first-call, and whether the Discord questions get dumber over the next two quarters. Dumber questions mean the hard parts stopped being hard.

Ask me in six months whether it worked.
