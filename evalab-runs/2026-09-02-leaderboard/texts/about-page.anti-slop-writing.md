We built Kestrel because of a whiteboard.

In 2019 a practice manager in Dayton, Rosa Alcántara, walked us through how her four-provider family clinic handled its day. Appointments went on a whiteboard behind the desk. Every evening she photographed it with her phone, in case the cleaning crew wiped a corner. Her clinic had paid for scheduling software two years earlier. Nobody used it, because logging one follow-up visit took eleven clicks and two screens that didn't talk to each other.

That's the problem we work on. Not scheduling in the abstract. The eleven clicks.

Kestrel does appointment booking, waitlist backfill, and reminder messaging for outpatient clinics with somewhere between two and forty providers. We're deliberately narrow. We don't do billing, we don't do charting, and when a hospital system asks us to build an enterprise resource module we say no and recommend somebody else. Small clinics get treated as a rounding error by most vendors in this space. We'd rather be the one company that doesn't.

Twelve of us, most in Columbus, four remote. Six came from clinical operations before they came here, which means the person answering your support ticket has probably worked a front desk during flu season and knows what 4:40pm on a Friday feels like.

We're HIPAA compliant and we'll send you the BAA before you ask. Our uptime last quarter was 99.94%, and the two incidents that cost us the rest are written up publicly on our status page, including the one that was entirely our fault.

Rosa is still a customer. The whiteboard's gone.
