A year of remote work taught us that our meeting calendar was load-bearing in ways nobody had noticed.

In the office, our engineers answered each other's questions by swiveling a chair. Remote, those questions went into Slack and sat there. Our median response time for a direct question in March was 47 minutes. By August we had cut it to nine, but only after Priya rewrote the on-call doc to say who answers what and put two names next to every subsystem.

We also killed our daily standup in month four and regretted it by month six. The standup was worthless as a status report, and we had been right about that. It was doing something else: it gave three people a slot to say "I'm stuck" out loud. When we removed the slot, they stopped saying it, and two of them burned a week each on problems Marco could have unblocked in twenty minutes. We brought back a fifteen-minute version, blockers only, twice a week.

The hardest part had nothing to do with tools. Our new hires took roughly four months to ship their first meaningful feature, against six weeks for the cohort we onboarded in the office. We fixed part of that by pairing each new engineer with one named person for their first month, someone whose calendar we actually cleared. The gap is now about ten weeks. We have not closed it.

We are staying remote. Our retention went up, and the people who left told us the commute was never coming back for them.
