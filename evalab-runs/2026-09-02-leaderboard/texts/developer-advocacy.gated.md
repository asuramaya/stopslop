# Why we're putting real money into developer relations

Our API has been public for two years. For most of that time our theory of adoption was simple: write decent docs, keep the status page green, and the developers will find us.

That held up until it didn't. Well over half of new signups never make a second API call. When we finally sat down and read a hundred of those sessions end to end, the failures were boring — a token scoped wrong, a pagination cursor that behaves unlike every other API the person has ever touched, a 400 whose message explains nothing. None of it shows up in a dashboard. It shows up in a Discord thread at 11pm, or it shows up nowhere at all, because the developer quietly goes and uses somebody else's SDK.

So we're funding a devrel team. Three people to start.

They are not marketing with a terminal open. The job is to sit where our users already are, write the integration guides the platform team never has time for, and carry the ugly feedback back to engineering in enough detail that nobody can wave it off. They get a standing seat in API design review and the right to file a P1.

We won't measure them on follower counts. We'll measure time-to-first-successful-call, and how many people are still building in week two.

Developers don't owe us their patience. Every hour one of them spends guessing what our error codes mean is an hour they aren't spending on us.
