We started the mentorship thing in March because our two newest backend engineers were both drowning in the same way, and neither of them wanted to ask the same question twice in standup. So I paired each with someone who'd actually shipped in that part of the codebase. That was the whole design.

I skipped most of what mentorship programs seem to come with. No matching survey, no goal templates. I did write down one rule: 45 minutes every other week, the mentee owns the agenda, and if there's no agenda you cancel. Guilt-free cancellation mattered more than anything else I set up.

The mentors got more out of it than the mentees, which I did not expect. Priya said walking someone through our deploy pipeline was the first time she noticed how much of it lived only in her head. She wrote it down afterward. That doc is what we hand every new hire now.

Two of the five pairs fizzled inside a month. That's fine. Making them continue would've taught the whole team that the program was theater, and people smell that fast.

The part I got wrong: I told everyone to bring "career questions." Nobody has career questions at week three. They have "why does this test only fail on CI" questions, and those are the ones that build trust. Once I said that out loud in the kickoff, the sessions stopped being awkward.

So if you're considering one, my advice is to not announce anything. Pick two people who'd benefit from talking. See whether they keep showing up.
