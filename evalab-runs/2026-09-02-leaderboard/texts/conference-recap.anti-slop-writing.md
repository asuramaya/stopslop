Four of us flew to DataCon in Chicago last week. Marcus lost his badge on day one and spent forty minutes at the registration desk, which turned out to be the best networking of the trip.

So what did we actually take home?

Mostly one thing, and it wasn't on any slide. Every team we talked to in the hallway track has quietly stopped trying to build their own evaluation harness. Three years of everyone rolling their own, and now people just pick something off the shelf and move on. Priya asked four different companies about it and got four versions of the same shrug. We've been building ours since March. That stings a little.

The talk on incident retros was good. Not revolutionary. The speaker, an SRE from a payments company whose name I keep mangling, made a point I've been chewing on since. Retros fail when the person who wrote the code runs the meeting. Obvious in hindsight. We do exactly that, every time, and I've never once questioned it.

Vendor floor was a wash. Apologies to whoever paid for the espresso machine booth. I drank four of them and I still can't tell you what your product does.

Two things we're changing. Retros get a rotating facilitator starting next sprint, and Priya volunteered to go first, which I suspect she'll regret by week two. The bigger one is that I want a full week spent deciding whether we keep the harness or kill it.

Nobody went to the closing keynote. We were at a bar on Randolph arguing about the harness.
