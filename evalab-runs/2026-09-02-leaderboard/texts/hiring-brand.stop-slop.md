## How we build

We ship on Tuesdays and Thursdays. Any engineer can push to production in their first week, and most do. Your onboarding buddy sits next to you for the first one and talks you through the rollback command before you need it.

We keep teams at four to six people. Each team owns its services from the design doc through the 3 a.m. page. No separate ops group picks up your mess.

Code review has one rule: answer within four hours of your working day. We argue in the pull request instead of in a meeting the following week. Reviewers point at the line they want changed and say what bothers them about it. When two engineers deadlock, they pull in a third and accept that person's call.

We write things down. An architectural change starts as a two-page document anyone in the company can open and comment on. We leave the rejected options in the document, so the engineer who joins in two years can see what we tried.

Fridays belong to you. Some people fix the test suite they have complained about for a month. Others read papers or build something with no ticket attached. We do not ask what you picked.

We hire people who push back in the interview. Half our infrastructure came from someone six weeks in who asked why we still did it the old way, then rewrote it.

You go on call one week in six. We pay for the sleep you lose, and we delete the alerts that wake you for nothing.
