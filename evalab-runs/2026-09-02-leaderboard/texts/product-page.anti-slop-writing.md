# Ember

Your team already has a chat tool. The question is whether it costs you twenty minutes every time someone asks "where was that decision made?"

Ember indexes threads the way a code search indexes a repo. Full-text, scoped by channel, filtered by date, and fast enough that you stop bothering to bookmark things. Search results show the surrounding ten messages, so you get the argument, not one line ripped out of it.

Incident channels spin up from a slash command and archive themselves with a timeline attached: who joined, what got pinned, when the page cleared. We built this because our own postmortems kept getting written from memory three days late.

What managers ask us about most, in order. Retention is configurable per channel, ninety days to forever. SSO comes with SCIM on every paid plan, not just enterprise. The GitHub and Linear integrations post state changes into threads instead of firing a new top-level message every time a label moves. Audit logs export as JSON. There's a real API, and the rate limits are published.

Things Ember doesn't do. No huddles or video. No app directory with four hundred half-maintained widgets. If your team lives in a voice channel all day, use something else, honestly.

Nine dollars per user per month. Import from Slack keeps threads intact, including the ones nested five deep, which took us longer to get right than anything else in the product.
