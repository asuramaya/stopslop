Our tools shipped fine last quarter, and nobody outside the building noticed. Six of us watched a support ticket come in from a developer who had spent two days reimplementing a function our SDK already exposed. She never found the docs page. That ticket is the reason we hired a developer relations lead in August.

The team makes something engineers have to adopt one at a time, by choice, usually on a Friday afternoon when a deadline is close. No sales call reaches that moment. A blog post might. A working code sample definitely does.

So we budgeted for two people who write, answer questions in our Discord, and sit in on customer integration calls. Their first job is the documentation, which our own engineers admit they skim rather than read. Their second job is to carry complaints back to the roadmap meeting, where someone with commit access can act on them.

We are not measuring this in impressions. Marcus, who runs the team, tracks time-to-first-successful-call for new accounts. It sits at 90 minutes. He wants it under 20 by March. If the number stays flat, we will have learned that the problem lives in the API design and not in how we explain it, which is worth knowing too.

Some of this looks like marketing, and finance asked about that in the budget review. The distinction we drew: marketing talks to buyers, and developer relations talks to the people who will maintain the integration two years from now. Those are different rooms with different questions.
