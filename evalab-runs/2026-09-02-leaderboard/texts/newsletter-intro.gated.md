Nobody schedules technical debt. It piles up the way laundry does — one reasonable shortcut at a time, each defensible on the Tuesday you made it, until the whole heap is load-bearing and you're scared to pull anything out.

This month we're writing about the heap.

The usual framing is guilt. We cut corners, we were sloppy, we'll pay it down next quarter. That framing is useless, and it's mostly not even true. Half of what we now call debt was the correct decision made with the information available, and the other half is just code that outlived the assumption it was built on. Neither is a moral failure. Both still cost you.

So the question isn't "how did we let this happen." It's narrower and harder — which of these do we actually have to fix, and how do we know? A migration that unblocks three teams is worth a quarter. A grim file that nobody has opened since 2023 is worth nothing, and rewriting it is a hobby.
