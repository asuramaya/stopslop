**Starting a mentorship program on an eleven-person team**

For most of last year, two people answered every question in our team channel. Sanjay and Wren. Not because nobody else knew the answers, but because they were the two who never made you feel stupid for asking.

That's a bottleneck dressed up as culture. So in March we made it explicit: everyone below senior gets a mentor, on the calendar, not optional.

Three things I got wrong.

I matched people by tech stack. The pairs that worked were matched on temperament instead: a nervous new grad with someone patient, an over-confident mid-level with someone who would push back. Stack overlap you can fix with a doc.

I made the sessions an hour. Half of them turned into status updates by minute twenty. Thirty minutes forces a real question.

And I gave the mentors nothing. No training, no time budget, no mention at review. Then I wondered why two of them quietly stopped booking. Mentoring is work. Fund it or it decays into a calendar invite people dread.

What worked was writing down what "ready for senior" means before we started, so the conversations had somewhere to go. Vague encouragement helps nobody.

Six months in, three people have shipped things they would not have volunteered for in January. Sanjay's channel volume is down about a third. That's the number I care about.
