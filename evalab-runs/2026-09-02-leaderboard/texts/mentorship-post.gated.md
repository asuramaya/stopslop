**We started a mentorship program. Here's what I got wrong.**

Six months ago three of our juniors quit within eight weeks of each other. The exit interviews all said a version of the same thing: nobody had time for them.

So we built a program. It's mostly working. Three things I'd change:

Pairing by tech stack was a mistake. I matched the Rust people with the Rust people. What the juniors actually needed help with was reading a codebase they didn't write, knowing when to ask for help, and deciding which review comments to push back on. None of that is language-specific. Round two I paired on temperament and it went better.

Thirty minutes a week is the floor, not the target. Everyone agreed to it in the kickoff. Half the pairs let it slide by week four, because a calendar invite with no consequence is a suggestion. It stopped sliding when the time showed up as a line item in the mentor's performance review and their manager knew it was there.

Mentors need mentoring too. Nobody arrives knowing how to run a 1:1 that isn't a status update. So we wrote them one page with three questions to open with, one rule — don't solve it for them — and explicit permission to say "I don't know, let's go find out."

Time to a new hire's first merged PR went from 11 days to 4. That's the number I can point at. Attrition under the two-year mark has been zero since March.

The part I can't measure is that people ask questions in the team channel now, instead of DMing me at 9pm.
