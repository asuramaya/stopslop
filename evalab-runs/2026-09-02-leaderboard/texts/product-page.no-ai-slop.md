# Frame — chat for engineering teams

Every reply lands in a thread. The channel stays a list of topics instead of a scroll, so you can read a week of #platform in about ten minutes.

Incidents get their own room type. Declare one from any message and Frame opens a channel, pins the timeline, and timestamps every status change. When you close it, the timeline exports as a markdown postmortem draft.

Code renders like code: syntax highlighting for 40 languages, diffs with expandable context, and stack traces collapsed to the first five frames until you click.

Search covers messages, files, thread titles, and the text inside pasted snippets, ranked by recency within the channel you're already in.

The parts your security review will ask about: per-channel retention you set yourself, SCIM provisioning, audit logs that export to S3 or Splunk, and a self-hosted build that runs on your own Postgres. SSO is on every plan, including the free one.

Frame has no video calls, no whiteboards, no document editor, and no app directory. It connects to GitHub, PagerDuty, Linear, Sentry, and Datadog, and stops there. If your team needs a meeting tool, you already have one.

Pricing is $6 per user per month billed annually. No seat minimums, no separate charge for guests, no enterprise tier that hides SSO behind a sales call.

Import a Slack export and read your own last six months in Frame before you decide. The free workspace keeps 90 days of history and never expires.
