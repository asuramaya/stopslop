# Welcome

You're here. Someone gave you a laptop, a badge, and probably a coffee mug with a logo on it. Now what?

First, don't try to be useful today. Really. Your onboarding buddy (check Slack, they've already messaged you) will walk you through the dev environment setup, and honestly that alone eats most of a morning because our Docker Compose file has grown weird barnacles since 2021 and nobody's brave enough to clean them off. If the local build fails on the auth service, that's normal. Ask. Someone in #eng-help has hit the same wall.

Week one is reading. We ask new engineers to ship a small change by Friday, usually a copy fix or a log message nobody loves, just so you touch the whole pipeline once: branch, PR, review, deploy. It's less about the change and more about proving the machinery works for you.

Things worth knowing early:

We do code review seriously here. Expect comments. They're not personal, and if a reviewer sounds curt it's because they typed it between meetings, not because you wrote something stupid.

Nobody works weekends. If you see a PR merged on Sunday, that person made a mistake and we'll talk to them about it.

Meetings have agendas or they get declined. Yours can too.

Your first three months, the most valuable thing you can do is ask the obvious question in public. The one you think everyone else already knows the answer to. Half the time they don't, and you've just saved four people an hour each.

Go find your buddy. Coffee's on the second floor, and the good machine is the ugly one.
