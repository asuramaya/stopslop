**Why we're funding a devrel team**

We lost a deal last quarter because a senior engineer couldn't get our SDK running in an afternoon. They didn't file a ticket. They didn't email sales. They picked the competitor, and we found out six weeks later, secondhand.

That's the whole argument.

Our product is an API. The buyer might be a VP, but the person who decides whether we survive the trial is whoever opens the docs at 11pm on a Tuesday. If the quickstart assumes a Python version we dropped two years ago, or the error message says `invalid configuration` and nothing else, we've lost before anyone talks to us.

So we've hired three people. Here's what they own. The quickstart gets tested on every release, on a clean machine, the way a stranger would run it. The top twenty support tickets each become either a doc page or a bug we actually fix. And they answer questions in public, in the forums and Discords our users are already in, including the questions that are embarrassing for us.

We're tracking one number to start: time from signup to first successful API call. It's eleven minutes today. We want it under three.

This isn't charity, and it isn't brand-building. Every hour an engineer spends confused is churn we've already paid for and can't see on any dashboard. Developer relations is how we make that hour visible, and then get rid of it.
