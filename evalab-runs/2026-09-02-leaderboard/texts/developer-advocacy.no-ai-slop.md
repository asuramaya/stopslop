We're hiring for developer relations, and I'd rather explain the reasoning than let people guess at it.

Start with the support queue. The three questions that come up most are all answered somewhere in our docs, and almost nobody finds those answers. People land on the API reference, copy a snippet, hit an auth error, and open a ticket. Our docs describe what every endpoint does without ever showing a working path from zero to a first successful call.

Then there's timing. We only hear from developers after they've already made up their minds. A ticket means someone got stuck. A churn survey means someone left. Nobody here has the job of sitting in the community Discord, reading issues on the third-party SDKs people built around us, and carrying that back before it turns into a reason to go elsewhere.

And our sales engineers keep getting asked for reference implementations, then building them one deal at a time. The same Stripe-plus-us integration has been written four times by whoever had a free afternoon.

So the role is concrete: write the quickstarts, maintain sample apps in the four languages our customers actually ship in, run weekly office hours, and file what they hear as product tickets with names attached.

We'll judge it on two numbers. Time from signup to first successful API call, which is currently about two days for teams that get there at all. And whether the top three ticket categories shrink by the end of the quarter.

If you've done this work and the queue-shaped version of it appeals to you, the posting is up.
