# Welcome

Your laptop's on the desk. The sticky note under it has a temporary password; change it before lunch.

Here's what today actually looks like. Someone from your team will find you in the first hour — if nobody has by ten, ping #eng-general and say so. You'll pair with them on setup, which takes most of the morning because our dev environment has three steps that are documented badly. We know. Fixing those docs is a decent first pull request, and plenty of people have made it theirs.

Aim to ship something small this week. Not a feature. A typo fix, a flaky test, a log line that never made sense. The point is to run the whole pipeline once while the stakes are low, so that when it breaks in month two you already know where the pieces are.

Two things about how we work. First, we'd rather you ask on day three than guess for a week; nobody here keeps score of questions. Second, if you break production, say so immediately and loudly. We don't do blame reviews. We do write things down afterward so the next person doesn't step in the same hole.

You'll get a mentor assigned this afternoon and a 1:1 with your manager on Thursday. Between now and then, read the architecture notes in the handbook, skim the last month of incident reports, and eat lunch with somebody you haven't met.

You were hired because of how you think, not because of what you already know about our stack. That part comes later.
