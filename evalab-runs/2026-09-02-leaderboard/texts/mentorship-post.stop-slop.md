We started pairing juniors with seniors last quarter after Priya asked why nobody had shown her how the billing service handled retries. She'd been on the team four months. She'd read the code three times.

The program is two rules. Each mentor meets their mentee for forty-five minutes a week, and the mentee sets the agenda. That second rule took the longest to sell. Senior engineers want to prepare a curriculum. They want to walk through the architecture diagram they made in 2023. The mentee doesn't need the diagram, they need to know why the retry queue has a hardcoded ceiling of 200 and who to ask before changing it.

We tracked one number: how long a new engineer waits before opening a pull request that touches something outside their onboarding ticket. Before, the median was eleven weeks. Now it's five.

Mentors get something too, though it took a few months to see it. Marcus rewrote half his team's runbook documentation because his mentee kept asking questions the runbook couldn't answer. He'd been maintaining those pages for two years without noticing the gaps.

Two things went wrong. We matched the first cohort by tech stack, which produced pairs with nothing to talk about outside code review. The second round we matched by what the mentee wanted to learn, and the conversations got wider. We also lost three pairs to calendar drift, where the weekly meeting slipped to biweekly and then vanished. Now the mentor owns rescheduling, and if a meeting moves twice in a row, their manager hears about it.

Start with four pairs. Six months of that will tell you more than a design document.
