## Vision

Two years from now, standing up a new service should cost an afternoon, not a quarter.

That is the whole bet. Today every team pays the same tolls over and over: wiring up auth, guessing at deploy config, rebuilding the same retry logic, discovering at 2 a.m. that nobody owns the alert. Each team solves it a little differently, and each solution becomes something someone has to keep alive. We are not short on engineers. We are short on engineers who aren't maintaining a private copy of the same infrastructure.

So we invest in the shared path — and we make it the fastest path, because a platform nobody chooses is just another team's backlog.

Concretely, by the end of the second year:

- A service goes from empty repo to production traffic in under a day, with observability, on-call routing, and rollback already attached.
- Product teams read the platform's docs, not its source. If they have to read the source, we've failed that quarter.
- The platform team owns uptime for the paved road. Product teams own their own code. The line is written down and we don't renegotiate it every sprint.

This costs real headcount, and it will slow some roadmaps in year one. We think that's the right trade. The alternative is what we have now, where the cost is invisible because it's spread across everyone, charged in small increments, forever.

We're not building a platform because platforms are good. We're building one because we can already name the work it deletes.

Before this circulates, be aware that the numbers and timelines are placeholders. Swap in your real onboarding times and headcount asks, since the argument only lands if the "cost we can name" is one your teams recognize.
