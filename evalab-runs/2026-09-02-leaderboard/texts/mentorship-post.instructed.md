## Starting a mentorship program on our team

We had six engineers in their first two years and four who'd been doing this a decade. The junior half kept getting unblocked, eventually, by whoever happened to be nearby in Slack. That's not mentorship, it's triage.

So we set something up. Nothing elaborate: pairs, a standing 45 minutes every other week, and a shared doc the mentee owns.

Three things I'd do again.

1. Let mentees pick. We published short blurbs from each willing mentor — what they work on, what they're bad at, what they like talking about. Mentees ranked their top three. Everyone got their first or second choice, and nobody felt assigned.

2. Give the mentee the agenda. If the mentor drives, it becomes a status meeting. We told mentees to bring one thing they're stuck on and one thing they want to be able to do in six months. The doc is theirs; the mentor writes nothing in it.

3. Separate it from the manager chain. No mentor is anyone's skip-level. Career conversations that touch compensation or promotion stay with managers. This one line prevented most of the awkwardness I expected.

Two things went badly. The first month, pairs cancelled constantly, so we moved the sessions onto calendars as recurring holds and cancellation dropped. And one pair just didn't click — we swapped them after eight weeks, which felt like a failure at the time and now reads as the program working.

Six months in, four of the six juniors are leading their own projects. I won't claim the program caused that. It didn't hurt.
