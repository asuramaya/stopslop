## Engineering culture

We ship small and often. Most changes reach production the day they're written, behind a flag if they're risky, and whoever wrote the code watches the dashboards afterward. There is no separate ops group to hand the pager to at 5pm.

Review is required and quick. Median time to a first comment on a pull request is under two hours, and we argue in writing, in the PR, where the next engineer can read the argument a year later. Seniority does not win arguments here. If you can't say why, it doesn't merge.

We are suspicious of process for its own sake. No sprints, no story points, no status meeting that could have been a paragraph. Each team keeps a one-page document of what it is doing this quarter and rewrites it when reality disagrees.

New engineers own something real in month one. Not a toy ticket — an actual service, with your name on the on-call rotation and the freedom to rip out the parts you think are wrong. Your first week is mostly reading, breaking things in staging, and asking the questions everyone else stopped asking two years ago.

We write a lot: design notes before big changes, postmortems after outages, and those are blameless in practice, not just in the handbook. Roughly a fifth of engineering time goes to paying down whatever the last quarter mortgaged.

We're about 40 engineers across three time zones, and we intend to stay small enough that you know everyone's name.
