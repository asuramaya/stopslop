Welcome to your first day.

Your laptop should already be on your desk with your accounts provisioned. If something is missing, message the #it-help channel and someone will sort it out within the hour.

Your manager blocked off an hour this afternoon to walk you through what the team owns and what it's working on this quarter. Before then, you have time to poke around. Clone the main repo, run the setup script in `docs/setup.md`, and see how far you get. If the script breaks, that's a bug in the script. File it. Two of our engineers found their first real contribution that way.

We expect your first pull request within the week. It doesn't need to be large. Fix a typo, delete some dead code, tighten a confusing error message. The point is to get through the pipeline once so you know how our review, CI, and deploy work before you need them under pressure.

A few things about how we operate. Code review here means someone reads your work and asks questions. Nobody rubber-stamps. If a review feels harsh, ask the reviewer what they meant. They will tell you.

We ship to production several times a day. You will break something eventually. When you do, tell the on-call engineer, then write up what happened afterward. We do not assign blame in those write-ups.

Ask questions in public channels rather than DMs. Someone else has the same question, and the answer stays searchable.

Your onboarding buddy is listed in your welcome email. They agreed to answer anything for your first month, including questions you think are too basic to ask.

Glad you're here.
