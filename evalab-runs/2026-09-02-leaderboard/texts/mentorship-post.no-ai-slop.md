Two new grads quit within a year of joining us. Both exit interviews said the same thing: no one had time to teach them anything.

We started a mentorship program in March. Six months in, here is what we set up and what I would keep.

Mentors come from a different team than their mentee. Same-team pairs kept sliding into code review, and mentees stopped raising the things they were embarrassed about. Cross-team pairs talk about career, politics, and how to disagree with a staff engineer in a design doc.

Mentors are never anyone's manager, or their manager's manager. Nothing gets back to performance review.

Forty-five minutes every other week, on the calendar, recurring. If the mentor cancels, the mentor rebooks. That one rule fixed most of our attendance problem, since the person with more power in the pair now carries the scheduling work.

The mentee writes the agenda in a shared doc before the meeting. No agenda, no meeting. It sounds harsh. It cut our "so, how are things?" sessions to almost none.

Terms run six months, then both people decide whether to continue. An expiry date makes it easy to end a bad match without anyone having a difficult conversation.

Fourteen pairs so far. Two dissolved early, both amicably. Three mentees have since asked to become mentors.

If you start one, write down the cancellation rule and the term length before you recruit anyone. Those are the two things we got wrong in the first month and had to fix in public.
