Nobody sets out to accumulate technical debt. It arrives the way clutter arrives in a garage: one reasonable decision at a time, each of them defensible on the Tuesday it was made. The deadline was real. The workaround did work. And then three years pass, and the thing nobody wants to touch is load-bearing.

This month we're writing about the code we step over on the way to somewhere else. Not to shame anyone, and not to launch a quarter-long remediation program that dies in week five. Mostly we want to be honest about the tradeoff, because "we'll clean it up later" is a promise we keep about as often as we keep the one about writing the runbook.

This issue carries a postmortem on the auth migration that took four attempts, a piece arguing that some debt is worth paying interest on forever, and a short guide to making the case for cleanup work when the roadmap is already full.

One theme kept surfacing as we put this together. The teams that handle debt well aren't the ones with the strictest rules. They're the ones where saying "this is a hack and here's why" carries no penalty.
