# A year remote: what we got wrong

We went remote in March, and the first thing we lost was the thing nobody had written down.

Our onboarding was never a document. It was a new hire sitting near someone for three weeks, overhearing why we don't touch the billing service on Fridays. Remote, that channel closed. Our fourth-month hire shipped nothing meaningful until August, and it wasn't their fault. We had never written the rules they were supposed to absorb. So we wrote them. Forty pages of it, painful, and overdue by about four years.

Second thing: meetings expanded to fill the calendar. By June the average engineer had 14 hours a week of scheduled calls, up from six. We killed standups and replaced them with a written thread. Nobody has asked for them back.

What surprised me is which parts didn't break. Code review got better, because people wrote real comments instead of leaning over a desk and saying "eh, ship it." Hiring got much better too. We stopped filtering on commute distance and made three of our strongest hires in cities we'd never looked at.

What did break was the twenty-minute conversation that used to happen because two people got coffee at the same time. We haven't solved that. We fly everyone in for four days a quarter, which helps for maybe three weeks afterward.

If I had to compress the year into a sentence: remote work doesn't create problems so much as strip away the padding that was hiding them.
