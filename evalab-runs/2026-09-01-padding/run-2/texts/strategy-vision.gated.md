## Vision

Two years out, a new service should reach production in a day, not a quarter.

That's the bet. Right now an engineer starting something new spends most of the first month on work nobody asked for: wiring auth, copying a CI config from whichever repo looked least stale, guessing at dashboards, filing a ticket to get a database. Median time from empty repo to first customer traffic is 34 days. About 60% of that is plumbing eleven other teams have already built eleven other ways.

We want to make that plumbing boring and mostly invisible. One paved path, owned by a team whose job is the path itself, with the escape hatches documented so that leaving the path is a decision rather than an accident. Product teams keep the parts that differentiate us and stop re-litigating the parts that don't.

This is a real trade. Platform work does not ship features, and for the first three or four quarters it will look like we slowed down. Some teams will lose autonomy they currently enjoy, including a few who use it well. We're accepting that because the alternative compounds against us: every quarter we defer this, the divergence between services grows and the eventual migration gets more expensive.

The measure we'll hold ourselves to is time-to-production for a greenfield service, tracked quarterly, with a target of under two days by the end of FY28. If that number isn't moving by the halfway mark, the strategy is wrong and we should say so.
