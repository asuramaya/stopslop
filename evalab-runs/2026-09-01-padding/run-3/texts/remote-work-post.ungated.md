# A year of remote work: four things we got wrong

We sent everyone home in March and called it temporary. It wasn't.

Meetings didn't shrink. They bred. By month six our engineers had 31% more calendar hours than the year before, because every hallway question turned into a scheduled call with an agenda. What fixed it was blunt: we deleted every recurring meeting on the books and made people re-create the ones they missed. A third of them never came back.

Onboarding broke first and broke loudest. New hires took a median 42 days to ship to production, against 19 the year before. Nobody was failing them on purpose. They just had no way to overhear things. We ended up assigning each new person a named buddy with two scheduled hours a week, and the number came back down to 24.

Writing turned into the actual job. The people who thrived weren't the loudest on video; they were the ones who could leave a clear paragraph behind at 4pm for someone starting their day in Berlin. We started reviewing design docs the way we review code.

And a warning, since we learned it the expensive way: async does not mean absent. Two people quit in the same month, both citing isolation, and neither had said a word about it in a standup. We now do a monthly 1:1 that has no agenda and no project talk.

Would we go back? No. But remote is a set of habits you have to build on purpose, not a place you move to.
