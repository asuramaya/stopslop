Nobody schedules technical debt. It shows up the way weeds do — a fixture copy-pasted instead of extracted, a TODO that outlives the person who wrote it, a feature flag kept "temporarily" since 2023.

We spent most of this month arguing about ours, and the argument turned out to be worth more than the conclusion. One camp wants a cleanup sprint. The other says a sprint is just a larger version of the habit that got us here: batch the mess, defer it, then take credit for the batch.

The metaphor doesn't help much either. Debt implies you borrowed on purpose and know the interest rate. Most of what slows us down was never a decision — it was a Tuesday.

So this issue skips the moralizing. Inside: a walkthrough of the migration we abandoned halfway and why the half-state was the expensive part; notes from the team that started deleting code on Fridays and what they learned about who actually owns a module; and a short piece arguing that some debt should simply be paid off never.

One request. When you read the postmortem on page four, resist the urge to decide who was careless. Ask instead what made the shortcut the reasonable choice at the time.
