**This month: the stuff we owe**

Nobody schedules technical debt. It arrives the way a credit card bill arrives — as the consequence of decisions that were correct when someone made them, made by people who were right to make them.

That's the part the retro usually skips. The standard framing says debt is what happens when engineers cut corners. Mostly it isn't. It's what happens when a deadline is real, a design is provisional, and shipping beats waiting for certainty you were never going to get. The corner-cutting kind exists. It's rarer than the folklore suggests, and much easier to spot.

The hard part is the interest. A module everyone quietly routes around. A test suite people rerun until it goes green. An onboarding doc with a section that begins "ignore this, it's out of date." None of that appears on a board. All of it gets paid, in hours, by whoever is unlucky enough to touch the file next.

So this issue is about saying the number out loud. Three teams walk through what they inherited, what they chose to fix, and what they decided to keep carrying — on purpose, with a reason they can defend.
