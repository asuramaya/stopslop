**About Waypost**

We started Waypost in 2019 after watching a family medicine office in Duluth run its schedule on a paper book, two whiteboards, and a phone tree. The book was accurate. Everything else was a guess.

Clinic scheduling looks simple from the outside. It isn't. A follow-up isn't a new-patient visit, a nurse can't take a slot that requires the physician, the room with the ultrasound machine keeps its own calendar, and half of it gets rebuilt when one provider calls in sick at 6:40 a.m. Most software treats those as edge cases. We treat them as the job.

Waypost handles appointment types, provider rules, room and equipment constraints, waitlists, and recalls. When a cancellation opens a slot, it texts the patients who said they could come in on short notice; clinics on Waypost fill about 60% of same-day cancellations. It writes back to Epic, athenahealth, and eClinicalWorks, so nobody at the front desk is retyping a thing.

We're 34 people, split between Minneapolis and Lisbon. Nine of us worked in clinics first — schedulers, medical assistants, practice managers — and they have veto power over any change that adds a click at the front desk.

We don't sell to health systems with a thousand providers. Our customers run 2 to 40, usually with one person who owns the schedule and knows most patients by name. That person is who we build for.

Support is staffed by people who've done the job, 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. Central. No ticket queue.
