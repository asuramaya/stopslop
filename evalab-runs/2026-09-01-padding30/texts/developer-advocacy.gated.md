**Why we're funding a developer relations team**

We hired our first developer advocate last month, and a few people asked why.

Here's the honest version. Our docs are fine. Our API is fine. And our activation rate (signup to first successful API call) sat at 31% for eleven months straight. We ran the usual experiments. We rewrote the quickstart twice, added a sandbox, and cut four fields off signup. It moved to 33%.

So we went and watched people use it. Six calls, screen shared, no script. Every person got stuck somewhere different, and none of those places were on our docs backlog. One spent twenty minutes fighting a webhook signature because our example pinned a library version we deprecated in 2024. Another quit when the error came back as `invalid_request` and nothing else.

You don't find that in a funnel chart. You find it by sitting with someone while they're annoyed.

That's the job. Not conference talks, though there will be some. The mandate is to be in the room where developers are struggling, come back with the specific thing that broke, and carry enough weight internally to get it fixed. The role sits under engineering rather than marketing, and the goals are activation and time-to-first-call, not impressions.

It might not work. Plenty of DevRel orgs turn into a travel budget with a podcast attached. But we've squeezed what we can out of dashboards, and the next ten points of activation are hiding in conversations we aren't having yet.
