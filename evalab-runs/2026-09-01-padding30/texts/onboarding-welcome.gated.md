# Welcome

Your laptop is on your desk. Your accounts should already work. If one doesn't, post in `#it-help` and someone will sort it out before lunch.

Here's what the day looks like.

**Morning.** Your onboarding buddy will come find you around 9:30. They'll get you through a checkout of the main repo and a local test run. That's the whole goal for day one. If the suite is green before you leave, the day went well.

**Afternoon.** Read `HANDBOOK.md` at the root of the repo. It's about twenty minutes and it explains how we branch, how we deploy, and who gets paged when something breaks at 3am. After that, pick anything tagged `good-first-issue` and open a pull request. It doesn't have to be a good one.

A few things worth knowing early:

We ship in small pieces. Most pull requests here are under two hundred lines, and that's on purpose. Big changes are hard to review and harder to roll back.

Ask questions in channels, not DMs. Someone else has the same question and won't ask it.

Nobody expects output from you this week, or next. Your first month is for reading code and figuring out where things live. Engineers who try to prove themselves in week one usually end up rewriting it in week six.

**Who to talk to.** Your manager has a standing 1:1 with you on Thursdays. Your buddy handles the day-to-day questions. For anything you'd rather not raise with either, Priya in People sits by the north windows.

Glad you're here.
