**We started a mentorship program and the first version was bad**

Here's what we did wrong: we paired everyone. Fourteen engineers, seven pairs, thirty-minute biweekly slots on the calendar. Very tidy.

By week five, four of the seven pairs had quietly stopped meeting. Nobody complained. They just let the invites lapse, which is what people do when a meeting has no purpose but declining it looks rude.

The problem was that we'd built a schedule instead of a reason. Two engineers sit down, and one of them has to invent a topic on the spot. That's a terrible ask. So the conversation drifts to "how's the sprint going," and after three rounds of that, everyone concludes the program is a waste.

Version two is narrower and it works. Mentees opt in with one written goal — an actual sentence, like "I want to lead the auth migration next quarter and I've never run a project across two teams." We match on that sentence, not on seniority. Pairs meet weekly for six weeks, then stop. If they want to keep going, they schedule it themselves.

The six-week cap did more than anything else. An open-ended commitment feels heavy, and people avoid heavy things. Six weeks is a thing you can finish.

Nine of eleven mentees finished the cycle. Three pairs kept meeting on their own afterward.

If you're starting one: skip the org-wide rollout. Take five volunteers, give them a deadline, and see what breaks.
