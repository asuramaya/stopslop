Nobody sets out to build technical debt. It arrives the way sediment does, layer by layer — a shortcut taken on a Thursday because the release was Friday, a TODO that outlived the person who wrote it, a config flag added for one customer and never removed. Each was defensible on its own. Together, they're why the deploy takes forty minutes.

This month we wanted to sit with that gap between individually reasonable and collectively expensive.

The metaphor is worth arguing with, too. Ward Cunningham coined it to describe deliberate borrowing. You ship now, you pay interest later, and you do it with your eyes open. Most of what we call debt isn't that. It's mess. It's code nobody fully understood at the time and understands less now. Calling it debt makes it sound like a financing decision when it was usually just Tuesday.
