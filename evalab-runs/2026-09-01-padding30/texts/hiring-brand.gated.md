## How we build

We ship small and often. The median pull request here is about 200 lines, it goes out the day it's approved, and the person who reviewed it is someone who has to live with the result.

Nobody owns a fiefdom. Engineers rotate through the parts of the system they never touched, because the alternative is a codebase held up by four people who each know exactly one thing. Expect your first month to include a change to something you weren't hired for.

On-call is real and it is shared. Every engineer carries the pager one week in eight, managers included. When something breaks at 3am, we write the postmortem the next day and we don't name a culprit in it. We do name the missing test.

We argue in writing. Anything that changes an interface or costs more than a week starts as a short design doc, two pages at most, open for comments for three days. Loud disagreement early is cheaper than a quiet rewrite in June. Once the decision lands, it lands, and we move.

We don't run on heroics as a substitute for staffing, we don't treat estimates as promises, and we don't keep a process document nobody has read since 2023. We keep meetings to two fixed afternoons so the rest of the week stays yours.

You'll write code that other people depend on within your first two weeks. You'll also delete some. Both count.
