Most clinic schedules break in the same place: the gap between what the calendar says and what the day actually does. A patient shows up late. A provider gets pulled into an urgent case. Two people at the front desk book the same room because one of them was on the phone.

[Company] builds scheduling software for that gap.

We started in 2019, after our founders spent a year sitting at the front desks of eleven family-practice and specialty clinics, mostly in the Midwest, watching staff work around their own software with sticky notes and a printed grid. What we built instead is a scheduler that models the things clinics actually run out of, including exam rooms, equipment, provider licensure by state, and interpreter availability. When the day goes sideways, it rebooks in seconds instead of making someone rebuild the afternoon by hand.

Today about 640 clinics use it, from two-provider practices to a 40-site orthopedic group. It is HIPAA compliant, connects to the major EHRs, and nobody has to leave the chart to move an appointment.

We are deliberately not a platform. We do not do billing. We do not do charting. We have turned down features that would have made us worse at the one thing we are for.

The company is 34 people, remote across the US, and roughly a third of us came out of clinic operations rather than software. That ratio is on purpose. When you call support, you get someone who has run a schedule.
