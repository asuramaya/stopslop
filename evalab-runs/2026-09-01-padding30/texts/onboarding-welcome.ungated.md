# Day One

Your laptop's on the desk. Sign in with the temp password taped to the lid, then change it — that's the first ticket in your queue.

Nobody expects code from you this morning. Here's how the day actually goes.

At 10, someone from your team grabs a room and walks you through the main repo. It's about 400,000 lines and nine years old. You will not understand it today. You won't understand it in a month either, and that's the normal state for everyone here, including the people who wrote it. Ask the dumb question. The person next to you had it last week.

Lunch is on the company Tuesdays. Otherwise the kitchen on 3 has decent coffee and a fridge people actually label things in.

Sometime this afternoon, you'll open a pull request. Something tiny — a typo in a log line, a missing null check. Your buddy already picked one out. The point isn't the change; it's watching it go through CI, get reviewed, and land in production before you go home. After that, the rest is repetition.

A few things worth knowing early.

Write it down. If you worked something out the hard way, put it in the docs while it still hurts.

Push back. If a design looks wrong to you on day three, say so on day three.

Go home at a reasonable hour. We don't staff heroics.

Welcome aboard. Find [your manager] if anything's missing.
