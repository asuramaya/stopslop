Six months ago our team had four engineers who'd been shipping alone for over a year, and two staff engineers whose calendars were mostly meetings about other people's work. So we paired them up.

The first version was wrong. We matched on tech stack — the person who knew Postgres best got the person who wanted to learn Postgres. That produced office hours, not mentorship. Questions got answered and nothing changed.

What fixed it was a different question at matching time: what do you want to be able to do in six months that you can't do today? One person said "run an incident without pinging anyone." Another said "argue with a principal engineer and not fold." Those are goals you can build a year around. "Learn Postgres" isn't.

The mechanics are boring on purpose. Thirty minutes, every other week, on the mentee's calendar so they own it. The mentee brings the agenda. If they don't, the session is cancelled, and nobody apologizes for it. We asked mentors to bring a real piece of their own work to the session at least once a month, no exceptions. Watching someone senior reason through a design they're genuinely unsure about does more than any advice they could give.

Two pairs fizzled by month three. We didn't rematch them — we ended them, told everyone we'd ended them, and said it was fine. Nothing kills a program faster than pretending the dead parts are alive.

Ask again in a year and I'll tell you whether it took.
