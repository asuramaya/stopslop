## How we work

We're about forty engineers. Nobody has "architect" in their title, and nobody will hand you a spec.

Most work starts as a one-page write-up: what's broken, what you'd do about it, what you'd give up to do it. People argue in the comments for a day or two. Then you build. We'd rather spend an afternoon disagreeing about the shape of a thing than six weeks unwinding the wrong one.

You own what you ship — the deploy, the dashboards, the pager. On-call is one week every two months. When a rotation gets loud, quieting it becomes real scheduled work, not a ticket that ages out of the backlog.

Every change gets reviewed, and reviews here are slow on purpose. A reviewer who only checks syntax hasn't reviewed anything. Expect questions about the thing you almost built instead.

A few things we don't do: no stack ranking, no whiteboard interviews, no reorg announced on a Friday afternoon. Salary bands are published internally, and we pay at the top of ours.

Friday afternoons are yours. A good chunk of our internal tooling came out of them, including the deploy CLI that everyone now runs a hundred times a day.

We're not a company that goes fast by staying late. Most people are gone by six. The work is hard in the way a good problem is hard, and then it's over, and you go home.
