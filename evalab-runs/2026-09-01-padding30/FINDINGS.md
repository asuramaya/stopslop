# The 30-prompt run: 2026-09-01

Thirty `padding` prompts, four arms each, `slopwatch`, generated by
`claude -p` (Claude Code 2.1.257), 891s wall clock at five workers. The
gated loop revised 19 of 30, so 19 rows carry signal and the other 11 are
a third independent sample of their prompt.

This is the run the earlier findings said was needed. Eight prompts gave
four signal-carrying rows and single-digit counts. This gives 19 rows and
counts in the twenties, which is enough to say something and still not
enough to say everything.

## What is established

**The gate eliminates exactly what it measures.** Enforced flags across
the 19: ungated 20, control 22, blind 22, gated **0**. Every document
reached zero. That is certain and it is also trivial, since the loop runs
until it does.

**It costs one extra generation per revised document.** 2.0 versus 1.0.

**It does not improve anything it does not measure.** Held-out flags
across the same 19: ungated 19, control 25, blind 16, gated 25. The gated
arm sits exactly where a second untouched sample sits. Whatever the loop
did for the checks it names, it did nothing for the rest.

That combination is Goodhart's law stated plainly. The proxy goes to
zero. The thing the proxy stands for does not move.

**No flattening.** Sentence-length variance across the arms: 9.19, 8.94,
8.91, 8.90. Flat. The monotone hypothesis found no support here either.

## What is suggested and NOT established

**That the gate is actively worse than a plain rewrite.** Held-out flags
at matched compute, same prompts, same run: blind 16, gated 25. Per 1000
words that is 3.35 against 5.02, and the blind rewrite is the only arm
that improved on the ungated baseline of 3.76 at all.

The direction is consistent. Per prompt, the blind arm carried fewer
held-out flags on 11, the gated arm on 4, and 4 tied. A paired bootstrap
puts the gate 1.64 flags per 1000 words worse, and 87% of resamples agree
on the sign.

It still does not reach significance. The sign test over 15 non-tied
pairs gives p = 0.12, and the bootstrap's 95% interval runs from -1.27 to
+4.45, which crosses zero. Nineteen rows are not enough for this
particular claim, and one prompt (`tech-debt-pitch`, 0 against 5) carries
a visible share of the gap.

So the responsible statement is that the gate shows no benefit beyond its
own checks, and every point estimate available runs against it, while the
stronger claim that it does harm remains unproven.

## The noise floor, which is the reason to distrust single comparisons

Two independent ungated generations of the same 19 prompts scored 3.76
and 5.13 held-out flags per 1000 words, 36% apart for no reason but
sampling. Any argument built on one arm against one other arm, without
matched compute and paired prompts, is inside that band. Only blind
against gated escapes it, because both are second-generation outputs of
the same prompts in the same run and differ solely in whether the
revision instruction named the flags.

## What would settle the open part

Roughly 60 signal-carrying rows, which is about 100 prompts at this
revision rate, or this same set repeated across several runs and pooled.
The instrument is ready for it: `--workers` makes 30 prompts a 15-minute
run, and the recordings here replay for free.

## Reproducing

```
python3 src/evalab/run.py --replay evalab-runs/2026-09-01-padding30/recordings --prompt-set padding
```

`texts/` holds all four arms for every prompt. No metric here decides
whether prose is good, and reading the blind and gated versions of
`tech-debt-pitch` side by side is worth more than any table above.
