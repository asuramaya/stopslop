For a team of three to six engineers with no dedicated infrastructure person, use scheduled batch runs against a Postgres table. A cron entry and a query that selects pending rows gives you retry, visibility, and an audit trail without a broker, a worker fleet, or a deploy story for long-lived processes. Run it every minute if you need lower latency; a one-minute cron plus `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` handles most of the workloads people reach for a queue to solve.

Move to a real queue when you hit a specific wall: a user staring at a spinner waiting on the job, a burst that a single run cannot finish before the next one starts, or work that needs to fan out across more machines than one cron host has cores. Those are observable conditions. You will know when you hit them, and until then the queue buys you latency you do not need and costs you a component that pages someone at 3am.
