For a team of three to five engineers, use the batch run. A cron entry plus a script that marks each row done covers reconciliation, report generation, cleanup, and email sends. It costs you no broker to patch, no worker fleet alive at 3 a.m., no second set of retry semantics to hold in your head. Write it re-runnable from the first commit and you have what a queue would have given you.

Add a queue the day a user is watching a spinner. If someone submits a form and needs the result in three seconds, cron cannot deliver that, and tightening the schedule to every minute does not change the answer. That is the line: batch for work with a deadline in hours, queue for work with a deadline in seconds. Nothing else on the list of reasons to adopt a broker holds up.

When you cross that line, use the infrastructure you already run. On Postgres, a jobs table with `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` is a working queue and costs you one migration. On Redis, use Redis. Do not stand up RabbitMQ or SQS for your first queue; you will spend the next year monitoring and upgrading it, and the outage will land on a Saturday. Buy latency when a user is paying for it, and buy it from the database you already back up.
