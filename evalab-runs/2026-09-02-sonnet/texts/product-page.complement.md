Team chat that stays out of your team's way.

Most chat tools optimize for message volume. Engineers end up with forty open threads, three separate places a decision might live, and a search bar that returns everything except what they typed. This one starts from a different question: what does a team need to remember, and how fast can they find it?

Channels map to your actual work structure — services, on-call rotations, incidents — not an org chart someone drew once and never updated. Threads collapse by default so a busy channel doesn't bury the message that mattered. Search indexes code blocks and file names, not just prose, so a stack trace pasted three weeks ago still turns up in ten seconds.

Integrations post where the work happens: a failed deploy lands in the channel that owns the service, tagged to the person who pushed it, not into a firehose everyone mutes. Permissions are scoped per channel from day one, so a new hire doesn't get read access to the incident channel from 2024 just because "public" was the easiest default.

For engineering managers, the cost isn't the subscription. It's the hours a team spends re-explaining decisions that were already made somewhere in the scroll. Retention policies, exportable history, and an admin console that shows who's actually reading what — not just who's online — mean you can answer "did we already discuss this" without asking around.
