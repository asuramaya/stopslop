We ship on a small team, so mistakes surface fast, and so does credit; code review happens in hours, not days, and if a PR sits overnight, someone asks why.

You touch production in your first week. We don't gate that behind tenure — we gate it behind tests and a second pair of eyes, and every engineer carries a pager rotation, including the people who wrote the original system, because nobody hands off the pain they caused.

We argue about architecture in writing, in the open, before we build. Design docs get real pushback instead of a rubber stamp, and if you're wrong, someone says so directly; you're expected to do the same back. That works because we separate the critique from the person, so disagreement doesn't linger.

Meetings are rare.

Most days you go heads-down for long stretches with Slack muted, and that's fine: we measure output, not presence. When we do meet, it's because writing it down first wasn't enough.

We keep a blameless postmortem culture, but blameless doesn't mean vague. Every incident review names the specific gap — a missing alert, a skipped test, an assumption nobody checked — and someone owns closing it before the next sprint. Refactoring is normal work, not a special request: see a mess on the way to your task, and you leave it better instead of filing a ticket and moving on.

If you'd rather ship something imperfect on Tuesday than something polished on Friday, you'll fit in here.
