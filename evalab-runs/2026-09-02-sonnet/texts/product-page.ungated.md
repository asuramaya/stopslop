**Relay — Chat that remembers why, not just what**

Engineering teams don't fail because they lack a chat app. They fail because the chat app hides the decision three scrolls back, buries the incident thread under GIFs, and turns every on-call handoff into an archaeology dig.

Relay was built for engineering managers who read chat logs to reconstruct what happened, not just to see what people said.

Every channel supports threaded decisions: pin the outcome, link the PR, and the thread closes itself out of your unread count. Search finds code blocks by language and error string, not just keyword matches. Incident channels auto-generate a timeline you can paste straight into the postmortem.

Relay integrates with GitHub, PagerDuty, and Linear directly, so a deploy, a page, and a ticket update land in the same thread instead of three different tabs. Permissions map to your org chart: you set who can create incident channels, who can @here a whole team, and who sees compensation-adjacent channels, before anyone asks.

For a 40-person team, that means fewer standups spent re-explaining what already happened in chat. For a 400-person team, it means the chat history holds up as a record you can actually audit six months later.

Compare Relay against Slack or Teams on three things: how fast a new hire finds the decision that shaped the current codebase, how much of an incident timeline you have to reconstruct by hand, and how many integrations actually reduce tab-switching instead of adding another notification source.

Fourteen-day trial. No credit card. Import your Slack history in one pass.
