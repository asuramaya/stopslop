We started Meridian because a front-desk coordinator at a three-doctor clinic told us she spent two hours a day on the phone just moving appointments around. That's the problem we build for: the daily friction of getting patients in front of clinicians without a scheduler drowning in double-bookings and no-shows.

Our software runs the calendar for clinics that can't afford a dedicated scheduling department. It syncs across providers and locations, sends reminders that actually cut no-show rates, and lets patients rebook themselves without a phone call. When a provider cancels a block of appointments, the system rebuilds the day instead of leaving staff to do it by hand.

We built the first version in 2019 for a pediatric practice in Austin after watching their staff juggle three separate spreadsheets. That practice still uses it. So do about 400 clinics now, from single-physician offices to multi-site specialty groups.

The team is small on purpose: engineers who talk to clinic staff every week, and a support group that answers the phone during business hours, not a ticket queue. Half of us have worked the front desk of a clinic ourselves, which shapes how we prioritize fixes. A dropped appointment costs a patient care and costs the clinic money, so scheduling bugs get fixed before feature requests.

We keep pricing flat per provider, with no per-appointment fees, because clinics need to predict their costs the same way they need to predict their day.
