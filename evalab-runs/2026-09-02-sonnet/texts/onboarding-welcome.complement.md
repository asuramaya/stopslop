Welcome. You're joining a team that builds tools people rely on, and today starts the part where you find out how we actually work.

Your laptop, accounts, and access should already be set up. If something's missing, ask your manager or drop a note in the #it-help channel — nobody expects you to solve that alone.

Your first two weeks are for orientation, not output. Read the onboarding doc your manager shares, meet your team, and get your local dev environment running. Ship something small in your first week if you can: a bug fix, a doc update, anything that gets a pull request through review. That's the fastest way to learn our tools and our standards.

A few things worth knowing on day one:

We review every change before it merges, including from senior engineers. A review comment is about the code, not about you.

We write things down. Decisions, incident notes, and design docs live in the wiki, not in someone's head. If you're stuck on where to find something, ask.

We move in small steps. Big rewrites and long-lived branches cause more problems than they solve, so keep changes small and merge often.

Your onboarding buddy will reach out today. They've done this before and can answer the questions that feel too small to bring to your manager. Use them.

You were hired because we think you'll make this team better. Take the time you need to get oriented. Nobody expects you to know everything by Friday.

Welcome aboard.
