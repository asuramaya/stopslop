Vision

Over the next two years we will treat platform work as a first-class investment, not overhead absorbed between feature deadlines. Every product team currently pays a tax: duplicated infrastructure, inconsistent tooling, and manual processes that should have been solved once. That tax compounds. The longer we defer platform investment, the more expensive it becomes to reverse. Our goal is a platform that lets teams ship product changes without re-solving deployment, observability, data access, and testing infrastructure each time.

Concretely, this means a small number of well-supported paths for common problems, clear ownership boundaries between platform and product teams, and internal tools built with the same rigor we apply to customer-facing systems.

This is not a rewrite for its own sake. We will prioritize the friction points that show up repeatedly across teams: slow build and deploy cycles, fragmented monitoring, brittle data pipelines. We will fix them at the root instead of patching symptoms locally. Where existing systems work, we will leave them alone.

Success looks like fewer surprises in production, faster onboarding for new engineers, and product teams spending more of their time on the problems only they can solve.

We will measure this through concrete signals: deploy frequency, incident recovery time, and the amount of engineering time spent on undifferentiated infrastructure work. Some feature work will move slower while platform teams build the foundation. That is a real cost, not a footnote. We accept it because the alternative — continuing to build product on infrastructure that fights us — costs more over a two-year horizon than it saves today. The teams doing this work will report progress against these metrics every quarter, not just at the end.
