Engineering here means owning a problem end to end, not a ticket queue. You pick the tools, decide the architecture, and carry the pager for what you built. Small teams ship to production every week.

We review code for correctness and clarity. If a PR sits for more than a day, raise it as a process failure.

Debugging a production incident matters as much as writing new features, and the person on call gets the same credit for a clean postmortem as for a launch. We write down what broke and why, then fix the root cause instead of patching the symptom.

You work across the stack when the problem requires it. A backend engineer who spends a week in the frontend to unblock a launch is doing the job.

Meetings are optional by default. If a decision needs a room, someone books one and writes down the outcome afterward so people who weren't there can act on it.

We hire for judgment, not years with a specific framework. Someone two years out of school who asks the right question in a design review carries the same weight as someone who has done this for fifteen years.

If you ship something wrong, say so, roll it back, and move on.
