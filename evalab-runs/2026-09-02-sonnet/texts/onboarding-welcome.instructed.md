Welcome. You're joining a company that builds software people rely on, and today you get access, a laptop, and a lot of unfamiliar context. That's normal. Nobody expects you to understand the whole system by Friday.

Your first two weeks are for orientation, not output. Set up your environment, get your accounts working, and read through the onboarding docs linked in your calendar invite. You'll be paired with a buddy from your team who can answer the questions that feel too small to ask in a group channel — use them.

By the end of week one, expect to have your development environment running and your first pull request merged, even if it's a small one. Small changes teach you the review process, the deploy pipeline, and who to ask when something breaks. By the end of month one, you'll have met your skip-level manager, sat in on a customer call or support rotation if your team does one, and picked up your first real project.

Ask questions in the open. If you're stuck for more than twenty minutes, post in your team channel instead of grinding alone — someone has hit the same wall before. Code review comments here are about the code, not you; take what's useful and move on.

Your manager will set up a 30-60-90 day check-in schedule with you in your first week. Bring questions to those meetings, including ones about how your role or team might change.

Look around. The people who onboarded you were new once too, and the documentation you'll edit next month is the same documentation that's confusing you today. Fix it when you find the gap.
