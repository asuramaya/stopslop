We started a mentorship program on our team this quarter, pairing six senior engineers with six newer hires for three months. The structure is simple: a weekly 30-minute call, a shared doc for questions that come up between calls, and one small project the mentee owns end to end.

The hard part was matching people. We didn't sort by tech stack. We asked mentees what kind of engineer they wanted to become and matched on that instead — one wanted to get better at debugging distributed systems, another wanted to learn how to push back on a bad spec. Matching on stack would have been easier and less useful.

We also made mentors write down what they wish someone had told them in their first year. Half the list was technical. The other half was things like "ask why a decision was made before you try to change it" and "your first PR review will feel harsher than it is."

Three months in, a few things are working. Mentees are asking questions in team channels they used to save for their mentor, which is the actual goal: expand the room, not create a bottleneck of one. The weekly call has been more useful than the doc; almost nobody writes in the doc between calls.

What we'd change: pick projects earlier. Two pairs spent the first three weeks deciding what to build instead of building it.

We're running a second cohort next quarter with the project picked before the mentor is even assigned.
