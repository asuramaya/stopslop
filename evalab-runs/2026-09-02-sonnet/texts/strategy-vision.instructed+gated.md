Platform work is the code that lets every other team move faster without thinking about it: build systems, shared libraries, deployment pipelines, internal tooling, data infrastructure. Over the past two years we funded it with whatever engineers had spare time. Builds are slow, services are duplicated, and onboarding takes weeks instead of days.

Over the next two years we will staff platform work with a dedicated team, give it its own roadmap, and hold it to the same planning rigor as product features. Every project on this roadmap ties to a measurable outcome: build time, deploy frequency, incident recovery time, or the number of teams able to ship without platform help.

This will feel slower before it feels faster. Migrating services onto shared infrastructure, retiring one-off tools, and consolidating deployment paths pull engineers off feature work for a quarter or two.

That cost is real, and we accept it because the alternative costs more. Every team rebuilds the same primitives, debugs the same class of production issues, and onboards new hires against inconsistent systems. We pay that cost already; it doesn't show up on a roadmap.

By the end of this period, a new engineer ships their first change in under a week. A team building a new service reaches for existing infrastructure by default, because it's faster than building their own. That is what this investment is for.
