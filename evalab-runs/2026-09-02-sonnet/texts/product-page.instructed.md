Focus, not features. Team chat that keeps engineering context intact.

Most chat tools optimize for volume. Threads fork, notifications pile up, and the message that mattered gets buried under twelve reaction emoji and a GIF. This one starts from a different premise: your team already has too much to read. The job is cutting that down.

Every channel maps to something real — a service, an incident, a repo — instead of a department nobody remembers joining. Threads collapse into a single card until someone opens them, so a busy channel doesn't drown people who dip in twice a day. Search returns the message, not a list of near-matches you have to click through one by one.

Deploys, PR merges, and on-call handoffs post as structured events, not walls of text from a bot. You see who shipped what, when, and whether it broke anything, without scrolling past a hundred "LGTM"s to find out.

Permissions default to least-access: a contractor sees the three channels they need, not your entire org chart. Message retention is configurable per channel, so incident postmortems can live forever while standup chatter ages out after thirty days.

None of this requires a migration weekend. Import from Slack takes an afternoon, and the pricing page has actual numbers on it — per-seat, no "contact sales" wall for teams under 200.

If your current tool's answer to "we have too many channels" is "add search filters," this is built for the moment you're past that.
