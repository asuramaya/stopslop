**How we work**

We hire people who'd rather ship something small and true than argue about something big and hypothetical. Most weeks look like: pick up a real problem, talk to whoever's closest to it, write code, put it in front of users. We keep the distance between "I have an idea" and "customers can try it" as short as we can make it.

We review each other's work, but not to gatekeep. If you can explain why an approach works, that's usually enough to move forward — we don't need three meetings to bless a decision one engineer already made carefully. Mistakes happen; we fix them and move on rather than building process to prevent every possible one in advance.

Nobody here owns a fiefdom. You'll touch the database schema on Monday and the onboarding flow on Thursday, because the person who understands the problem best should be the one solving it, not whoever's on the roster for that layer.

We write things down — decisions, tradeoffs, the reasoning behind a weird workaround — because the next person (often future-you) shouldn't have to reconstruct it from git blame. Documentation is part of the work, not an afterthought tacked on before a launch.

Remote-friendly, async by default. We trust people to manage their own time and don't measure engagement by hours online. What we do care about: did the thing get better this week, and can you tell us why you built it the way you did.

If that sounds like your kind of team, we'd like to talk.
