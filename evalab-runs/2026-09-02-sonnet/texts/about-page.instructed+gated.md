We started Meridian because a friend who runs a three-doctor family practice spent four hours a week untangling double-booked slots by hand. That was 2021. Her front desk had a whiteboard, a paper calendar, and a scheduling tool built for restaurants.

Clinics book differently than everyone else. A follow-up needs the same doctor as last time. A new patient intake needs forty minutes, not fifteen. A no-show in an urgent-care slot costs more than one in a routine checkup, and a provider on maternity leave can't just vanish from the calendar without someone reassigning her patients first. We built Meridian around those constraints instead of bolting them onto generic booking software.

The product handles multi-provider calendars, insurance-aware intake forms, and waitlists that fill a cancelled slot automatically, and it syncs with the major EHR systems clinics already run so front-desk staff aren't re-entering the same patient twice.

Clinics using it report shorter phone queues and fewer missed appointments. The number that matters most to us is simpler: staff stop dreading Monday mornings. We're a team of fourteen, split between Austin and remote, with three former clinic administrators on staff who test every feature against how their old front desks actually worked. No venture-backed growth targets force shortcuts here, and no enterprise sales cycle dictates the roadmap. We serve independent and small-group practices, the ones large hospital systems often ignore.

Meridian now runs scheduling for more than 600 clinics across 38 states, and we're still adding features one clinic request at a time, the way we started.
