**From the editors**

Every team carries debt it didn't mean to take on. A shortcut from a deadline three quarters back. A dependency nobody remembers approving. A test suite that passes because half of it stopped running months ago. This month we're looking at technical debt not as a moral failing but as a normal cost of building things under real constraints — and asking what it actually takes to pay it down without grinding everything else to a halt.

Inside, one team walks through how they mapped a decade-old billing system before touching a single line of it, and what they found when they finally did. Another writes about the argument for leaving some debt alone — not every shortcut is worth the interest it costs to fix. We've also got a rundown of the refactoring tools that earned a permanent place in people's workflows this year, and a shorter piece on how to talk about debt with a product team that just wants the feature shipped.

None of this is about chasing a debt-free codebase. That doesn't exist. It's about knowing what you owe, and choosing when to pay.
