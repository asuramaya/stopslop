Welcome. You're joining a team that ships code other people depend on, and today you get accounts, a laptop, and a lot of names you don't know yet.

In your first week, get your development environment running and land one small change. Ask your onboarding buddy for the setup doc, and ask questions in the team channel rather than guessing at config files.

Read a few recent pull requests before you write your own. Pay attention to naming patterns, review habits, and how commit messages are written — the patterns you see there matter more than anything in this document.

Every pull request needs at least one approval before it merges, and reviewers here comment on reasoning, not just syntax. Code review is where you'll learn the most. Push back when you disagree; a reviewer who's wrong wants to hear it.

Your manager will set up a 1:1 with you this week. Bring questions, half-formed ideas, and anything that's confusing about the product or the team.

Your accounts should work by end of day one. IT sits on the third floor if a badge or laptop needs fixing, and the team wiki has an FAQ for common setup problems that's often faster than asking someone directly.

You don't need to understand the whole system before you contribute. Small, working changes teach you more than a week of reading ever will.
