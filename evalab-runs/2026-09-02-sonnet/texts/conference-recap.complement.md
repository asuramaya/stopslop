Team Recap: What We Took Away From SaaSDay 2026

Four of us went to SaaSDay in Austin last week, and the biggest shift in our thinking came from a session on pricing, not the product talks we expected to learn the most from.

The pricing panel argued that most teams set tiers around what's easy to build rather than what customers actually value. One speaker showed usage data from three companies that moved to consumption-based pricing and found revenue per account rose 20-40% within two quarters, mostly because heavy users had been capped by flat tiers. We're now pulling our own usage logs to see if the same pattern holds for us.

On the engineering side, a talk on incident response stuck with us. The presenter's team cut mean time to resolution in half by writing runbooks in plain, procedural language instead of prose, so an on-call engineer at 3 a.m. can follow steps without parsing paragraphs. We're rewriting our own runbooks this month.

We also spent time at the expo floor talking to smaller vendors rather than the big booths, and found two tools worth a trial: a load-testing service and a customer feedback aggregator that pulls from support tickets, reviews, and sales calls into one queue.

The weakest part of the conference was the keynote track, which stayed high-level and skipped the numbers we came for. Next year we'll prioritize the breakout sessions over the main stage.
