Threadline

Most engineering teams already have a chat tool. The question is whether yours makes decisions easier to find or easier to lose.

Threadline ties every conversation to the thing it's about: a PR, an incident, a deploy, a ticket. Reply to a GitHub comment from the app and it posts back to GitHub. Open an incident channel and it links to the postmortem doc automatically. Six weeks later, when someone asks "why did we do it this way," the answer is one click from the code, not a scroll through a general channel.

Search covers messages, code snippets, and file names in one pass. Find a Terraform variable someone pasted in March as fast as yesterday's standup notes.

Notifications default to quiet. A channel muted at 2pm stays muted until you unmute it. No digest emails, no "40 unread" badge that trains people to ignore the app. In a study of 40 teams that moved off always-on notification tools, engineers logged 3 fewer context switches per day.

Threadline runs on-call rotations, incident channels, and PR review requests as first-class objects, not bots bolted onto a generic chat API. Set an on-call schedule once and it handles paging, handoff notes, and escalation without a separate tool.

Pricing is per active user, not per seat. If half your team logs in twice a week, you pay for half your team.
