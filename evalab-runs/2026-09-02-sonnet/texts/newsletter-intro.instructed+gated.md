Technical debt is this month's theme because three teams asked for it in the same survey cycle.

Every codebase carries loans it took out under deadline pressure. The interest compounds quietly until a routine change takes three times longer than it should, and someone finally asks why. This issue is about naming that cost accurately, not moralizing about it.

We open with a piece from the payments team on the migration they shelved twice before finally scoping it as a two-week project instead of an open-ended cleanup. Debt gets paid off when it's scoped like a feature, not when it's scheduled as a favor to future engineers. Next, a walkthrough of how one team tracks debt in the same issue tracker as bugs, with severity labels that force a conversation about tradeoffs instead of a vague backlog item nobody owns.

We close with a shorter piece on the debt you can't refactor away: decisions baked into a system's boundaries, where the fix is a rewrite, not a patch. Worth reading if you've ever inherited a service and wondered why nobody just fixed the obvious thing.
