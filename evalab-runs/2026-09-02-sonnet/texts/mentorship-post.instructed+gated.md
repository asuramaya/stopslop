We started a mentorship program on the team this quarter: six pairs, four months, one rule. Mentees pick the topic each session.

The pairing process took longer than expected. We matched on stated interests first, then swapped two pairs after the kickoff call revealed a scheduling conflict. Next round, we'll check calendars before matching on skills.

The first few sessions ran long on status updates.

We fixed that by giving each pair a one-page prompt sheet — questions like "what's a decision you made this week you're unsure about" instead of "how's it going."

One mentee, three weeks in, asked her mentor to review a design doc before it went to the team. That doc shipped with two fewer review rounds than her last one.

Not every pair found their rhythm. One pair meets biweekly and mostly talks about career goals. Another meets weekly and spends half the time on code review. Both count as working, since the program never set an agenda beyond the first sheet.

We're extending the program a second quarter and adding three more pairs. The prompt sheet stays. The forced weekly cadence goes: two of the six pairs asked to switch to biweekly, and we should have let them from the start.
