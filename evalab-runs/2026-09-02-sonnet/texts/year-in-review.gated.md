**Year in Review: Engineering**

We shipped fewer features than last year, and that was the point. The team spent the first two quarters paying down debt in the payments pipeline instead of adding to it. We rewrote the retry logic, killed three cron jobs nobody remembered starting, and cut our on-call pages in half. Nobody wrote about it at the time, so we're writing about it now.

The second half looked different once the pipeline stopped catching fire. We built the things people had been asking for since last January: real-time inventory sync, the export tool finance had wanted for two years, and a new onboarding flow that cut setup time from twenty minutes to four. None of that would have landed on the old foundation.

How we work changed too. Code review went from a bottleneck to a same-day expectation, mostly because we agreed to review smaller chunks. Incident retros stopped producing blame and started producing follow-up tickets that got closed.

We missed a deadline on the export tool by six weeks because we underestimated the migration. We had one outage in March that cost us a weekend. Each taught us something we needed to know.

Next year we're carrying two things forward: more reliability work, because it kept paying off, and the search rewrite we've deferred twice already.

Everyone who did the unglamorous parts made this year work. Most of the job is unglamorous parts.
