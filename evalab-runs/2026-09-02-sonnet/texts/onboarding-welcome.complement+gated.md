Welcome to the team.

You'll spend your first week getting your laptop set up, meeting your manager, and reading through the docs your team has written for people in exactly your position. Take your time with it.

Ask questions in your team channel even if they feel basic. The person who answers you learned the same way, from someone who answered their questions.

Your onboarding buddy will reach out before your start date. They're not your manager and won't be grading you. They're the person you message when you're stuck on something small and don't want to interrupt a meeting to ask.

By the end of week one, you should have your development environment running and have shipped a small, low-risk change to production. Shipping something real, even something tiny, teaches you more about how the codebase and the deploy process work than a week of reading would. Nobody expects you to understand the whole system by Friday. Most engineers here are still learning parts of it years in. If a system looks confusing, it probably is, and the person who wrote it will tell you why if you ask.

We review each other's code, and you should expect the same in return. A review comment is not a judgment of you — it's someone trying to catch a bug before a customer does.

If something in this process is broken, tell your manager. Onboarding gets better because new hires point out what didn't work for them. Welcome aboard. We're glad you're here.
