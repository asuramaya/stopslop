We built this because clinic scheduling is still run on phone trees and paper triage sheets that were designed for a slower era of medicine.

Front desk staff spend hours a day matching patients to the right provider, room, and time slot, then calling to confirm, then calling again when something changes. A single canceled appointment can trigger five phone calls and an empty slot that never gets filled. We watched this happen at clinics we worked with and decided the fix wasn't a better phone system. It was removing the need for most of those calls.

Our software reads a clinic's real constraints — provider hours, room availability, equipment requirements, insurance rules — and builds a schedule around them automatically. When a patient cancels, the system finds the next eligible patient on a waitlist and offers them the slot by text. When a provider runs behind, downstream appointments shift without a staff member touching the calendar. Clinics using our platform fill 94% of same-day cancellations, up from an average of 60% before switching.

We started in 2019 with three former clinic administrators who had spent a combined 40 years scheduling patients by hand. That background shapes how we build: every feature ships after we've tested it inside a working clinic, not just a demo environment. We now support scheduling for 1,200 clinics across 38 states, from single-provider practices to multi-site health systems.

Our team is split between engineers and people who worked the front desk. That mix keeps the product honest — a scheduling feature that looks elegant on a whiteboard has to survive a Monday morning with three no-shows and a provider running 40 minutes late.
