This month we're writing about technical debt.

Every team carries it. The question is whether you can see it, price it, and choose when to pay it down. A shortcut taken under deadline pressure last quarter is now the reason a routine change takes three days instead of one. That's the trade: speed now, cost later, and the interest rate depends entirely on how well you tracked what you owe.

This issue looks at three teams handling that trade differently. One paid down a decade-old data model before it caused an outage, not after. Another built a dashboard that scores debt by blast radius, so priority decisions stop being arguments about opinion and start being arguments about numbers. A third describes when they decided not to pay off a known shortcut, because the system it lived in was scheduled for replacement anyway.

None of these are stories about avoiding debt. Debt is a normal tool, the same as a loan is a normal tool for a business. The failure mode isn't taking it on. It's forgetting you owe it, or refusing to ever pay it back.
