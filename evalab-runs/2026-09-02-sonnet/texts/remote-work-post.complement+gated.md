A year ago we shut the office and told everyone to work from wherever they had a chair and a wifi signal. We expected a dip in output and a slow crawl back to normal. Neither happened the way we predicted.

Meetings were doing less work than we thought. Cutting the standing weekly sync and replacing it with a written update in Slack saved four hours a week per engineer, and nobody missed the meeting. We now default to async updates and reserve video calls for decisions that need real back-and-forth. Our onboarding process depended on hallway osmosis we never wrote down, too: new hires who joined remotely in the first few months struggled to find basic answers, because those answers lived in people's heads, not in docs. We spent the spring rewriting our internal wiki, and time-to-first-commit for new engineers dropped from three weeks to eight days.

Some of our best collaboration happened in person, by accident, and no tool replaces it. That argued against everything else we were doing, which made it the hardest thing to admit. We now fly the whole engineering team in twice a year for a week of planning and pairing. Attendance is optional but almost everyone comes, and the two trips have become the weeks people plan their vacations around.

Revenue held steady through the transition, and turnover dropped as people stopped commuting and started keeping their evenings. The office lease is gone. Most of the team says they will not go back to five days a week even if we ask, and a few have said they would leave if we did.

We are not claiming remote work fixed everything, but it forced us to write down what used to live in the walls, and the writing turned out to be worth keeping.
