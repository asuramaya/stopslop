Starting a mentorship program on our engineering team

We paired every junior engineer with a senior one for six months, one hour a week, no fixed curriculum. The mentor picked whatever the mentee was stuck on that week: a PR review, a system they hadn't touched, a career question.

The structure was deliberately thin. We tried a syllabus in the first attempt two years ago and it died within a month because nobody had time to maintain it. This time the only rule was that the hour happened, and that the mentee set the agenda.

Three mentees shipped their first cross-team project during the program. Two asked for a second round with a different mentor once the first ended. One pairing didn't work and we reassigned it in week three rather than forcing it through to month six.

The main cost wasn't the mentors' hour. It was the ramp-up time before each session, since a mentor who hadn't looked at the mentee's code that week showed up with nothing useful to say. We ended up asking mentees to send a short note the day before: what they were stuck on, what they'd already tried.

We're running a second cohort in October with eight pairs instead of four. The mismatch we saw once will happen again at that scale, so we're building in a week-three check-in instead of waiting for someone to flag it.

If you want to start one on your team: skip the curriculum, pick pairs based on what the mentee is actually working on, and check in early enough to fix a bad match before it becomes six wasted months.
