Welcome to your first day.

Your laptop is on the desk. The password is on the sticky note under the keyboard, and you should change it before lunch. Your manager will find you around ten, after standup, and walk you to the coffee machine that works. The one on the second floor does not.

You will spend today reading. Nobody expects code from you this week. Clone the main repo, run the setup script, and when it fails on step four, ask in #eng-help. It fails for everyone on step four. We have not fixed it because the fix keeps breaking on Linux, and someone rewrites the script every quarter and gives up.

By Wednesday, pick a small bug from the "good first issue" list. Ship it Friday. Your reviewer will leave forty comments. That is normal here, and it happens to people in their fourth year too. We argue about code and then go to lunch.

Some things worth knowing early. Deploys go out twice a day, at 11 and at 4, and you press the button yourself once you have shipped three changes. If you break production, say so in #incidents right away. We write up what happened without naming anyone as the cause, because the person who pushed the button is rarely the reason the button was dangerous.

Ask questions in public channels instead of DMs. Somebody else has the same question and will find your thread in six months.

Your onboarding buddy is listed in the email we sent you Monday. Bother them. That is the whole job description for the next two weeks.

We are glad you are here. Go find the coffee.
