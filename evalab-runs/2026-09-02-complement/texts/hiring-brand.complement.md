## How we build

We ship small changes often. Most pull requests here run under 400 lines, and the median one merges the day it opens. Two engineers review every change: one for correctness, one for whether the code will be easier to work in next month.

We write things down. Any decision touching more than one team gets a short design document, usually two pages, circulated before the work starts. The documents live in the repository beside the code they describe, so the reasoning stays with the thing.

On-call rotates weekly across everyone who ships to production, managers included. When a page fires twice for the same reason, fixing the cause jumps ahead of the roadmap. We run retros within three days and publish the notes internally, including the ones that make us look bad.

New engineers merge to production in their first week. Your onboarding buddy stays with you for a month and is judged on how quickly you stop needing them.

We argue in writing and in the room, then commit. The person closest to the problem makes the call. If you think a call is wrong, bring a concrete alternative and a rough cost.

A fifth of each quarter goes to work engineers pick themselves: paying down debt, build tooling, dependency upgrades, whatever has been grinding on you. About half of last year's biggest latency wins came out of that time.

We work remote-first across eight time zones and meet in person twice a year. Meetings get recorded, documents are the default, and no real decision happens in a hallway.
