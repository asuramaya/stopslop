# We started a mentorship program, six months in

Three people left the team last year and all three said some version of the same thing in their exit conversation: they didn't know who to ask. Not about architecture decisions, about the smaller stuff. Whether a PR comment was a blocker or a suggestion. How much time to spend on a flaky test before escalating. Whether it was okay to say "I don't understand this codebase yet" in week nine.

So in March we paired every engineer with under two years here to someone with more than four. Thirty minutes a week, standing, on the mentee's calendar so they own the cancellation.

What worked: making it explicitly not about performance. Mentors don't write feedback for review cycles and they don't report to the mentee's manager. That separation took one meeting to establish and it's the reason people talk honestly.

What didn't: our first matching pass paired by tech stack. Turned out the useful pairings crossed teams. A backend engineer mentoring someone on the mobile side has no stake in their sprint, which makes the conversation wider.

We also underestimated the load on senior people. Four engineers ended up with three mentees each because they were the ones who volunteered. We capped it at two and recruited more mid-level mentors, who turned out to be better at the week-nine questions anyway since they remembered having them.

Six months in, retention is up but the sample is too small to claim causation. The thing I can point to: junior engineers now open PRs with questions in the description instead of waiting for someone to find the problem.
