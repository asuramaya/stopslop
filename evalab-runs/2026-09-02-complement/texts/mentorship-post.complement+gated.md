We started a mentorship program on my team last quarter. Here's what we learned.

The trigger was a pattern in exit interviews. Three of the four engineers who left in the past year said some version of "I didn't know what I was supposed to be getting better at." Nobody mentioned pay, and two of them had been on the team over two years. So we paired every engineer below senior with someone at least one level up, outside their immediate project, and that last detail carried more weight than anything else we decided. When your mentor is also the person reviewing your PRs, the conversation stays technical and stays safe. Cross-project pairs talked about career shape, about which projects to say no to, about how to disagree with a staff engineer in a design review.

We set two rules. Thirty minutes every two weeks, on the calendar, mentor's responsibility to reschedule if it slips. And no agenda template, which we tried in week three and which turned every session into a status update.

Six months in, seven of nine pairs are still meeting.

The two that stopped both said the same thing: the mentee didn't have questions. I think that's a real failure mode rather than a personality mismatch. Someone three months into a job has questions. Someone three years in has often stopped asking them, and a biweekly thirty-minute slot won't restart that on its own. If I ran it again I'd start the mentees with one concrete thing they want to be able to do in a year. The pairs that had that never ran out of material.
