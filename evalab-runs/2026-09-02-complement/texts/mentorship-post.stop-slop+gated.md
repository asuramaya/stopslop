Six months ago I handed my director a one-page mentorship proposal. She approved it in the meeting. That was the easy part.

My first version failed. I matched people by tech stack, told them to meet every two weeks, and left them alone. Four of the eight pairs stopped meeting by week six. The mentees gave me the same answer in different words when I asked why: they had nothing to bring. A junior engineer sits down across from a staff engineer, gets asked "so what do you want to work on," and freezes.

The second version gave them something to bring. Each mentee picks one skill per quarter and writes it as a task they want to do without help. Two people picked reviewing a database migration. One picked running an incident as commander. The mentor then finds real work that exercises the skill and reviews the result with them, so the meeting has an artifact in it.

Mentors needed a reason too. I asked our director to name mentorship in the staff-level promotion criteria, and she did. Three people signed up the week after that went out.

The program costs each pair an hour every two weeks, plus about four hours a month from me on matching and check-ins. Seven of nine pairs are still meeting after two quarters. One pair I dissolved at the mentee's request, and I matched her with someone else.

If you start one, skip the kickoff deck. Write down what each mentee wants to be able to do, then go find them the work.
