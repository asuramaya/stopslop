# Why we're hiring for developer relations

We shipped an API two years ago and mostly stopped talking about it. Adoption grew anyway, which was flattering and also a problem: we had no idea what people were doing with it. Support tickets were our only signal, and support tickets skew toward the moment something breaks. That gave us a picture of our product assembled entirely from its failures, which is a strange way to run a company.

So we're building a developer relations team. Three people to start, reporting into product rather than marketing. That reporting line is deliberate. We don't want a team whose job is to generate impressions.

The first thing they'll do is unglamorous. Our quickstart takes 40 minutes to get to a first successful call, and about a third of people abandon partway through. We know this from instrumentation, not intuition. Fixing that is worth more than any conference talk we could give this year.

After that, the work is mostly listening: sitting in on integration calls, reading the Discord instead of skimming it, writing down the questions that come up over and over and turning them into either docs or, more often, API changes. Our webhook retry semantics confuse nearly everyone who touches them, and no amount of prose will fix a design problem. There's a business case too, and it's ordinary. Developers evaluate tools before anyone signs a contract. If evaluation is painful, we lose deals we never hear about, and fixing that is cheaper than sales.

We're hiring two developer advocates and a docs engineer. Postings go up next week.
