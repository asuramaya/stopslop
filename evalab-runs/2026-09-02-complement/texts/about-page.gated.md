# About Slate

Slate makes scheduling software for medical clinics. That's the whole product. No billing module, no charting, no assistant that drafts your patient emails.

We started in 2019, after our founder spent a year and a half consulting for independent practices and kept walking into the same scene: a three-provider clinic running its day out of a paper book and a whiteboard, next to a $40,000 system that could technically handle scheduling. It handled forty other things too, badly, and took nine months to configure. Most offices gave up and kept the whiteboard.

So we set one bar. A receptionist should be able to book, move, or cancel an appointment in under five seconds, on a seven-year-old machine, without reaching for the mouse. Everything else in Slate follows from that. Keyboard-first. Under a second to load on a slow office line. Keeps working when the connection drops, and syncs when it comes back.

About 1,400 clinics use Slate today, mostly independent practices with two to twenty providers, plus a few dozen community health centers. We're 31 people in Providence. We're profitable, which means we answer to the clinics paying us rather than to a board that wants a billing module by Q3.

Our support line is staffed by former clinic schedulers. Call it and you'll reach someone who has personally run a Monday morning with two providers out sick.

We publish our prices. No annual lock-in, and we'll export your data the day you ask for it, in a format you can open.
