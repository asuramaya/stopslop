Keel — team chat with an off switch

Keel is built the other way around from every chat tool you have tried.

Start with what chat costs you. The average engineer on a 30-person team gets pinged 41 times a day, and each one takes about 12 minutes to recover from. Most tools sell you more of that and call it collaboration. In Keel, channels are quiet by default. Notifications go out on a schedule you set per person, per channel, so an on-call alert wakes someone at 3am and a design thread does not. Anything urgent gets an explicit override, and Keel logs who used it. Managers can see that log. It turns out people ping less when the interruption has their name on it.

Threads have a lifespan. Set one to close in 48 hours and it archives itself into a summary your team can search, instead of scrolling forever with three unresolved questions buried in it.

The integrations you care about are there — GitHub, PagerDuty, Linear, Jira, Sentry, plus a webhook API that takes about 20 lines to wire up. Self-hosting is supported on day one, not as an enterprise upsell. So is SCIM, SSO, and an audit trail that satisfies SOC 2 without a plugin.

No video calls. No org chart, no document editor, no AI that summarizes your standup. Other tools do those. We would rather do one thing without asking your team to live inside it.

Free for teams under 10. $6 per person after that. Import from Slack takes about an hour.
