**About Wardline**

Wardline schedules patients for outpatient clinics that run two to forty providers.

Dana Okafor managed the front desk at a Sacramento family medicine group for six years. She rebuilt the same broken workflow four times: a nurse blocks an hour for a procedure, the software double-books it anyway, and a patient waits in the parking lot at 7:40 a.m. for an appointment nobody entered. Dana and Miguel Torres, who wrote billing systems at Epic, started Wardline in 2019 to fix that one problem.

The product does a narrow set of things. Your schedulers see provider availability, room availability, and equipment holds on one screen. The system reads your intake forms and gives a 40-minute slot to a diabetic foot exam instead of the default 15. A patient cancels at 6 a.m., and Wardline texts the three people on your waitlist who live within twenty minutes of the clinic, then hands the slot to whoever answers.

We charge per provider per month, with no implementation fee and no annual contract. Clinics leave when they want to, and we hand back your data in a format your next vendor can read.

Forty-one people work here, most of them in Sacramento and the rest at home desks across six states. Eleven of us worked in clinics before this. Call support at 2 p.m. on a Tuesday and you reach someone who has stood at a check-in window through flu season.

We hire nurses who want to write software. Dana reads every application herself: dana@wardline.com.
