We make scheduling software for medical clinics.

Dana Whitcomb and Ari Belov started the company in 2019, after six years running the front desk at a pediatric practice in Providence. They spent their mornings on the phone rebooking patients who had arrived to find another family sitting in their slot, and their afternoons apologizing in the waiting room. The vendors selling to practices their size offered products built for hospital systems with an IT department, priced for one too.

About 900 clinics use Clearday now. Most have between two and eleven providers, and they run family medicine, dermatology, physical therapy, dental. Our software handles the parts of scheduling that break at that scale: a provider who splits the week across two locations, a patient who needs 40 minutes when the intake form says 15, the Monday after a holiday when 60 people call before ten.

We employ 34 people. Eighteen of them have worked in a clinic. Call support and you reach someone in Providence who can open your schedule while you talk, and who knows what a 22 percent no-show rate does to a practice's month.

We charge per provider, per month, and the number sits on the pricing page. We take no cut of your billing. We sell your patient records and your appointment volumes to nobody, and the contract says that in language you can read without a lawyer.

Bring us the schedule your front desk currently repairs by hand, and we will show you what our version does with it before you pay us anything.
