# A year without an office

We gave up our lease last March, mostly because it was expiring and nobody wanted to argue about it. Twelve months on, here is what held up and what didn't.

Hiring changed first and changed most. We used to draw from a forty-minute radius. Now four of our eleven engineers live in places we would never have posted a job, and two of them are the strongest hires we have made.

The thing we assumed would break didn't. Code review, standups, shipping: all fine, sometimes better, because a written thread beats a hallway conversation that only two people heard.

What broke was everything unscheduled. New people took months to work out who to ask about anything. A junior developer sat stuck on a config problem for two days that someone would have solved by leaning over a desk, and we had no idea until the sprint review.

So we started paying for the accidents on purpose. Every new hire gets a named buddy for their first six weeks and a standing thirty-minute call, agenda or not. We fly everyone in twice a year and spend the time on arguments rather than presentations. Docs went from a chore nobody did to the only way a decision survives past the week it was made.

Remote did not make us faster. It made us better staffed and worse at the informal stuff, and we spent a year building scaffolding to replace what the office had been giving us for nothing. We are not going back to the office. We have stopped calling the switch free.
