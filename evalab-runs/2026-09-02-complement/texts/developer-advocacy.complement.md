We're hiring a developer relations team this quarter, and I want to explain the reasoning rather than announce it.

Last year we shipped an API that we thought was self-explanatory. Our docs covered every endpoint. Support tickets said otherwise: 40% of them were people who had read the docs and still built the wrong thing. The docs described what each call did. Nobody had written down how the calls fit together for the three or four jobs people actually came to us for.

That gap is the work. Developer relations, done well, means someone whose job is to build with our product the way a customer would, hit the same walls, and then either fix the wall or write the guide that gets people past it. That person has to be senior enough to file a pull request against the SDK, not just a bug report about it.

The other half is upstream. Our roadmap is currently shaped by whoever escalates loudest through sales. Developers who churn quietly at the integration stage never show up in that signal. We want a team that sits in the Discord, reads the GitHub issues nobody triaged, and brings the pattern back to engineering with enough detail to act on.

We're measuring this on time-to-first-successful-call and on integration-stage churn, not on conference talks or follower counts. If those two numbers don't move in twelve months, the approach was wrong and we'll say so.

Two roles are open now: one engineer-facing, one docs-and-education. Both report into engineering, not marketing. That placement is deliberate.
