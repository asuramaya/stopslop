## About

We build scheduling software for medical clinics. That is the whole company.

Meridian started in 2017 in a two-doctor family practice in Bend, Oregon, where our founder was working as a practice manager and rebuilding the next day's schedule by hand every evening. Three no-shows, one provider out sick, a double-booked ultrasound room. The software the clinic already owned could store appointments. It could not help her put them back together.

So the first version of Meridian did one thing: it took the day apart and proposed a new version of it, with the constraints a clinic actually has. Provider licensure. Room and equipment conflicts. The rule that a Medicaid follow-up cannot be pushed past a certain window. Interpreter availability, which almost no scheduling tool models at all.

Today about 1,400 clinics use us, most of them independent practices between two and forty providers. Median no-show rate among our customers dropped from 14% to 9% in the first year on the product; we publish the methodology behind that number on our metrics page, including the clinics where it did not move.

We are 31 people, remote across the US and Canada, with roughly a third of the team having worked in a clinic before working here. We are profitable and have taken no outside investment, which means we answer to the practices paying us rather than to a return schedule.

We are not trying to become your EHR. We integrate with the one you have, and we would rather be the small piece of your day that stops being a problem.
