We hired two developer advocates in March and gave them a travel budget larger than our sales team's. Two board members asked me to defend that.

Start with a support ticket from last year. A backend engineer at a payments company spent six days wiring up our webhooks before he gave up and wrote to us. Our docs had the retry semantics wrong. One paragraph, wrong for eleven months. My team had not opened that page since we shipped it, because none of us needed it. He needed it, and he came close to leaving.

Sales cannot fix that paragraph. Marketing cannot fix it. The person who fixes it has to sit with our own API long enough to hit the walls our customers hit, then care enough to file the correction.

That is the job I am paying for. Priya and Marcus spend their weeks building throwaway integrations and sitting in our Discord, watching people fail at the parts we assume are obvious. Last month Priya rewrote the auth quickstart after she watched four people in a row paste the wrong key. Signups that reach a first successful API call went from 31 percent to 48.

I want to be plain about the accounting. I cannot draw a line from a conference talk to a signed contract, and I am not going to pretend otherwise. I can point to a shorter path between "I heard about you" and "it runs on my machine." I can point to engineers who recommend us in Slack rooms we do not sit in.

We are funding two more roles next quarter.
