**A year without the office**

We gave up the lease in March and told everyone we'd revisit it in three months. The review kept sliding, and eventually it stopped being a question anyone asked. A year in, here's what we actually learned, including the parts we got wrong.

Meetings multiplied before they shrank. For the first four months our calendars filled with recurring syncs that existed to replace hallway conversation, and they were worse at it. We cut standups to twice a week in July and nothing broke. Engineering hours went up by roughly six per person per week, mostly recovered from meetings we should never have booked.

Writing turned out to be the load-bearing skill. Decisions that used to survive as shared memory in a room now had to live in a document or they didn't live at all. We started requiring a written proposal before any architectural discussion. It slowed the first week of every project and saved us arguing the same points in month three.

Onboarding was where we struggled. New hires who joined pre-March had a network to draw on. The ones who joined in year one took about twice as long to reach their first independent ship, and two of them told us in exit interviews that they never felt like they knew anyone. We now pair every new engineer with a named buddy for eight weeks and fly the team together twice a year.

We're not going back to an office. We are much less confident than we were in June that remote work is free.
