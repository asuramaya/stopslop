Our two newest backend engineers had shipped nothing larger than a config change in ten weeks. They asked sharp questions in standup, then went quiet for days. I started the mentorship program to fix that.

The first version failed. I matched six pairs by tech stack, told them to meet monthly, and waited. Four pairs never met. The two that did spent an hour on career advice neither person wanted to give.

The second version worked because I cut the ambition. Each mentor pulls one ticket from their own queue and hands it to the mentee. They sit together for the first forty minutes. The mentee finishes the work alone, and the mentor reviews the PR line by line with reasons attached to each comment. Thirty minutes a week, on the calendar, and the mentor owns the slot.

Two warnings before you copy this.

Mentors need cover. I pulled one project from each mentor's quarter and told my director why. If you skip that step, your mentors will drop the sessions in week three and blame themselves for it.

Match on working style over stack. My best pair was a Go engineer teaching a Python engineer, because both of them think out loud and neither minds an argument. My worst pair shared a codebase and never got past politeness.

By June both new engineers were reviewing other people's code. One of them now mentors the intern we hired in July. I still run the program myself, and I still read every pairing before it goes out.
