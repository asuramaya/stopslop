# Why we're funding a developer relations team

Last quarter we watched a support ticket sit open for nine days. The customer had wired our webhook signing wrong, following a code sample from our own docs that had been stale since the v2 migration. They eventually gave up and switched to a competitor. Nobody on the team was assigned to notice.

That ticket is the argument for this hire, or rather these three hires. We are staffing a developer relations team starting in October.

The job is not conference talks. The first six months are docs triage, sample code that actually runs in CI, and someone whose full-time work is reading what developers say about us in places we don't control. Our API has grown by roughly 40 endpoints in eighteen months, and the documentation has been maintained by whoever shipped the endpoint, in the hour before they shipped it. That model got us here. It will not get us to ten thousand integrators.

We also want the feedback loop pointed inward. Engineers who build the API are poor judges of whether the API is usable, because they already know the answers. A developer relations team sits in the gap and reports back, with specifics: which error message sends people to Stack Overflow, which auth flow takes four attempts.

The budget comes out of paid acquisition. We spent a lot last year buying attention from developers who then hit our getting-started guide and bounced. Fixing the guide is cheaper.

We'll publish what we learn, including the parts that make us look bad.
