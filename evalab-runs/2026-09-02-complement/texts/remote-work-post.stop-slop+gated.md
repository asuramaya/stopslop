We closed our Toronto office last March. Forty-one people went home with a monitor and a promise that we would sort out the rest.

We got meetings wrong first. We kept the old calendar and bolted video onto it. By June our engineers sat in fourteen hours of calls a week and shipped less than they had from desks. Priya killed all but two of the recurring meetings and told teams to write instead. Our release cadence moved from every three weeks to every nine days.

Writing costs more up front. A design doc eats an afternoon. The doc outlives the argument, though, and Tomas reads it in Lisbon at 9am without asking anyone to repeat themselves. We now require a written proposal before any decision meeting, and readers cancel about half of those meetings once they see the doc.

We broke onboarding and left it broken for four months. New hires had nobody to overhear. Since September we pair each new engineer with a buddy for six weeks and hand them a first ticket that forces them into two unfamiliar services. Time to first merged PR dropped from eleven days to four.

We tried virtual happy hours and dropped them, since eight people attended in week one and two attended in week six. We also killed a Slack bot that paired strangers for coffee. Nobody wants a fifth call.

We fly the whole company to one city twice a year now. Those four days cost us $180,000 and buy the trust that carries the other 361.
