## How we work

Engineers here own what they ship. The person who writes a service is the person who gets paged when it breaks at 3am, and the person who decides whether the fix is a patch or a rewrite. We keep on-call rotations to one week and cap them at six people, so nobody is carrying a pager every third week.

Code review is mandatory and usually fast. Median time to first review is under four hours during working hours. Reviewers are expected to explain a rejection well enough that the author could have reached the same conclusion alone.

We write things down. Any change to a system boundary starts as a short design document, circulated for a week, argued about in comments rather than meetings. Most of these are two pages. A few have run to twenty when the decision was expensive enough to deserve it.

Meetings are opt-out. If you are in a recurring meeting that has stopped being useful to you, leave it and say why. Managers are told not to treat this as a problem.

New engineers ship to production in their first week, usually something small with a real user on the other end. Your onboarding buddy is on the hook for making that happen, and it is part of their review.

We hire people who disagree in writing and change their minds in public. The trade we are offering is real autonomy over your technical decisions in exchange for real accountability when they turn out wrong. If that sounds like a bad deal, this is probably not the right team.
