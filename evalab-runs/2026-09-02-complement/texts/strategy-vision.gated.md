## Vision

Two years from now, a new engineer should be able to ship a production service on their first week without asking anyone how deploys work.

That's the whole bet. Right now every team carries its own copy of the same problems — build pipelines that drifted apart in 2023, four ways to get a secret into a container, three incompatible opinions about how a service reports its health. Each copy was reasonable when someone wrote it under deadline. Together they cost us roughly a third of engineering time, spent re-solving problems that were already solved two desks over.

We want to pull that shared work into a platform other teams consume as a product, with an owner, a roadmap, and users who can file bugs against it. It's a paved road rather than a mandate. If a team goes off it, that should be a deliberate choice with a reason attached, and the road should still be the easy option.

By end of FY27 we want one deployment path, one observability stack, and service scaffolding that produces something running in under an hour. Self-serve for the ordinary case. A human in the loop only when something is genuinely unusual.

None of this shows up as a shipped feature, which is why it keeps losing to whatever is loudest that quarter. So we're stating the trade openly. We will move slower on product for the next four to six quarters, and we expect to make it back in the two after that — through teams that stop maintaining private infrastructure and start doing the work we hired them for.

One note on the numbers. The one-third figure, FY27, and the four-to-six-quarter dip are placeholders; swap in whatever your own data supports, since a vision section that cites a made-up number is worse than one that cites none.
