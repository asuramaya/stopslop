We closed the office in March and told forty-one people to work from home for two weeks. The two weeks lasted a year.

The first month went fine. We shipped on schedule, our standups ran shorter, and two engineers moved out of the Bay Area and cut their rent in half. I told the board that remote had cost us nothing.

By June I was wrong about that. Our junior engineers stopped asking questions. A new backend hire spent eleven days stuck on a deploy script that any of us could have fixed in four minutes at a desk. He didn't want to interrupt anyone on Slack. He quit in September.

We changed how we work. Every new engineer gets a named person to bother, and that person's job description says so. We run a thirty-minute open call at 10am where you can show up with a broken build and nothing else. Attendance is optional and half the team comes.

Meetings got worse before they got better. We had eleven recurring calls in August. We killed seven and wrote the decisions into a doc instead. Nobody has asked for them back.

I underestimated how much of our old process ran on someone overhearing something. Our designer used to catch bad specs because she sat two feet from the PM. We pay for that now with a written review step that adds two days to every project.

We're staying remote. Retention is up and I hire from four time zones. It costs more to run than the office did, in ways that never show up on a lease.
