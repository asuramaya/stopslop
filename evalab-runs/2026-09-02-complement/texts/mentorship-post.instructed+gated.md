# Starting a mentorship program on our team

We paired every engineer under two years of experience with a senior for six months, one hour a week. Six months in, here is what happened.

The first version failed. I matched people by tech stack, wrote a doc with suggested discussion topics, and assumed the meetings would happen. Four of the nine pairs stopped meeting by week three. When I asked why, the answer was the same each time: neither person knew what the hour was for, so it was the easiest thing on the calendar to cancel.

The second version worked because we gave the hour a job. Each pair picks one thing the mentee wants to be able to do by the end — run an incident, write a design doc that survives review, get comfortable in the billing service. The session is about that, and the mentor's job is to hand over work that builds toward it. Abstract career conversation is welcome, but it is not the point.

Two things I would tell anyone setting this up. Mentors need time, not encouragement. We moved two mentors off on-call rotation for the six months, and both of their pairs are still meeting. And let people opt out at the halfway mark without explaining themselves. Three pairs did. That is a functioning program, not a failing one — a bad match costs more than no match.

Seven of the nine original pairs are still active, mentees rate the sessions 4.1 out of 5 for usefulness, and two of them now review code in services they had never touched in January.
