We started pairing juniors with senior engineers in March. Six months in, here's what I'd do differently.

The first version failed. I made a spreadsheet, matched people by tech stack, and told everyone to "meet regularly." Three pairs met once. One pair never met at all. The problem wasn't motivation. It was that nobody knew what the meeting was for.

So we gave it a shape. Every pair gets a standing 45 minutes on Thursday, and the mentee owns the agenda. They bring something: a PR they're nervous about, a design doc they can't start, a conversation with a staff engineer that went sideways. The mentor's job is to react, not to prepare a lesson. That one change took our show-up rate from 40% to about 90%.

Two other things mattered more than I expected.

We stopped matching by language. A Go mentee with a frontend mentor turned out fine, because the questions people actually bring are rarely about syntax. They're "how do I push back on this deadline" and "is it normal to feel this slow."

And we capped it at two mentees per mentor. Senior engineers say yes to everything, then quietly drown. Making the limit explicit gave people permission to decline.

What I still can't solve is measurement. I have attendance numbers and a survey nobody trusts. The evidence that it's working is anecdotal: two engineers told me they stayed because of it. I'll take it.

Start smaller than you think. Four pairs, one quarter, then ask them what broke.
