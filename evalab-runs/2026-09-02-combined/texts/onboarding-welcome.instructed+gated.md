# Welcome

You're here. Someone will hand you a laptop, a badge, and probably too many browser tabs.

Today is mostly logistics. Your buddy — you'll meet them by 10am — walks you through account access, the VPN, and where we keep the things that aren't written down anywhere. They've done this walk for every engineer who joined this year, so the gaps in it are known gaps. Ask them the dumb questions now, while you have the excuse of being new. In a month you'll feel silly asking where the staging database lives, and you'll still not know.

## The first two weeks

We don't expect output. Read code, sit in on standups, build a mental map of the systems. Ship something in week one if it happens; nobody is counting.

By the end of week two, most engineers have made a real change to a real service. Your buddy will point you at a starter task that touches a few systems on purpose, so you see how they connect.

## How we work

Code review is required and usually fast. Design docs come before big changes, and they're short. On-call starts after your third month, never before, never alone the first time. Most decisions happen in written threads you can search later, though the search rarely goes well on the first try. When you can't find why something is the way it is, ask in the team channel — the answer is usually a person, not a document.

## If something is broken

Setup instructions rot. When a step in the onboarding guide fails, fix the guide. That's your first contribution, and it counts.

Take your time today. Go home at a reasonable hour.
