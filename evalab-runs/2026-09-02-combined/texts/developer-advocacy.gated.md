# Why we're hiring a developer relations team

We shipped our API eighteen months ago and assumed the docs would do the rest. They didn't. Here's what we saw in the funnel: roughly 40% of new keys were used exactly once, then never again. The support queue filled up with questions our reference pages technically answered, just not in a place anyone looked, and not in the words anyone searched. Nobody was angry. They just quietly went and used something else. That's the gap devrel fills, and it isn't marketing. Marketing gets a person to the signup page. Devrel is about what happens in the twenty minutes after, when someone is in their own terminal with their own weird stack and our example doesn't compile.

So we're hiring three people.

One will own the getting-started path end to end, with a hard target: working request inside ten minutes, on Windows too. One will spend most of their week outside the company, in issue threads, on calls with the teams already building on us, writing down what breaks. The third will maintain the client libraries we've been shipping as an afterthought. We're measuring this on time-to-first-call, second-week retention, and how many support tickets turn into doc changes. If those don't move by summer, we got the bet wrong and we'll say so.

We built something developers have to be talked into. Talking to them is the job.
