Our third engineer quit in March. In her exit interview she said she had shipped for fourteen months and could not name one person who had sat down and read her code with her.

I paired six people the following week, with a single rule. An hour every other Tuesday, and the mentor brings the agenda.

That rule came out of a failure. When I tried this in 2021, I let the pairs figure out their own format, and within a month the meetings turned into status updates. Mentees arrive with nothing because they think their questions are too small to take an hour of a staff engineer's time. A mentor who walks in with a topic hands them permission to ask.

I matched on the work rather than the ladder. Priya owns payments, so she took the two engineers whose tickets land in payments. They already talk in code review. The mentorship hour gave them somewhere to have the conversation that a review thread cannot hold, like why we settled on idempotency keys instead of the thing everyone wanted to build.

Two pairs fizzled by June. One mentor went on leave and I did not backfill him fast enough, and the other pair told me they were friends already and this felt like homework. I stopped scheduling them.

I track two small things, whether the meeting happened and what the mentee shipped in the quarter. Four of our six mentees have merged something they would have escalated to a senior a year ago. Ask me again in a year whether that holds.
