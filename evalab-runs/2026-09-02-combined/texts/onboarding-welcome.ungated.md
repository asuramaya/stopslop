# Day One

Welcome. Your laptop is on your desk, already imaged, and the sticker drawer is by the kitchen — take two.

Here's what today actually looks like. Your onboarding buddy will find you around 10, walk you through badge and VPN, and then leave you alone until lunch. That gap is on purpose. Clone the monorepo, run `./bin/setup`, and watch it fail somewhere around minute six. It fails for everyone. The error message tells you which Homebrew package is missing; fix it, then open a PR against `docs/setup.md` so the next person hits a different wall than you did.

That's the whole culture in one exercise. We don't have a wiki page called "Our Values." We have a rule: if something confused you, you're the last person it gets to confuse.

Your first real ticket is already assigned. It's small — a copy change in the billing export, maybe forty lines with tests. Ship it this week. Nobody expects architecture opinions from you in September.

Some practical things. Standup is 9:45 and lasts nine minutes. Wednesdays are meeting-free and we defend that. If you're blocked for more than thirty minutes, say so in your team channel; asking early reads as good judgment here, not as struggling. Deploys go out about eleven times a day and yes, you'll break production eventually. So has everyone who wrote the runbook you'll use to fix it.

One ask for your first two weeks: keep a note of everything that seemed strange. You'll stop noticing by October, and that list is worth more than you think.
