# About Kestrel

Kestrel makes scheduling software for medical clinics. Dana Okafor and Miguel Ruiz started the company in 2019, after four years at a six-provider family practice outside Tacoma. Dana ran the front desk there. Miguel maintained the billing integration that broke each time a provider joined. The two of them kept a paper waitlist next to a $40,000 practice management system, because the software refused to put a nurse visit and a physical in the same hour.

Miguel wrote a replacement over one winter. An office manager at a clinic down the road asked for a copy, and then two more did.

About 1,400 clinics use Kestrel today. A receptionist in Boise moves a patient to Thursday in two clicks, and the system notifies the referring specialist. Your office manager adds a new appointment type without calling us. We employ 62 people. Eleven of them worked a front desk or a billing office before they came here, and we send our engineers to sit in waiting rooms for two days during their first month. You learn more from one Monday morning at a pediatric practice than from a quarter of user interviews.

We charge per provider, per month, and we publish the price on the pricing page. We do not sell patient data. We sign a business associate agreement before you send us anything. If you decide to leave, you export your schedule and your history in a format you can open. Our support line reaches a person in Portland between 6 a.m. and 7 p.m. Pacific.
