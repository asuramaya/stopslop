# Welcome. Glad you're here.

Your first job is not to be useful. It's to get oriented, and orientation takes about three weeks — nobody here expects otherwise.

Today, do two things. Get the codebase running on your machine. Then ship something tiny, a typo fix, a stale comment, a log line nobody loves. It goes to production. That's the point. You will learn more from watching one small change move through review, CI, and deploy than from a week of reading architecture docs.

Some things worth knowing early.

Ask fast. The unwritten rule is fifteen minutes. Stuck longer than that, post in your team channel. Nobody here scores points for suffering quietly, and the person you're afraid of bothering was you, eighteen months ago.

Reviews are about the code. Someone will leave forty comments on your first pull request. It isn't hazing. We're loud about the work and careful with the people, and if that line ever gets crossed, tell your manager. That's a real thing you can do, not a sentence in a handbook.

You will break production. Probably in month two. We run blameless postmortems because the failure is a property of the system, not of your character. Bring what you learned. Leave the apology at the door.

Your manager has a 1:1 on your calendar for this afternoon. Bring questions, including the dumb ones. Especially those.

The coffee on the third floor is better than the coffee on this one. Nobody will tell you that, so I am.
