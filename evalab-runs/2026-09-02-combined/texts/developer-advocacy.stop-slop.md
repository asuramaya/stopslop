We hired two developer advocates last month, and I want to explain the reasoning to the people funding it.

Our docs get 40,000 visits a month. Support tickets tell me a third of those readers leave without an answer. I read fifty tickets in July. They repeat six questions, and every answer already sits on a page nobody finds.

Sarah joined from Stripe in June and spent her first week rewriting the quickstart. Time to a first successful API call fell from 25 minutes to nine. She shipped no features. She deleted three pages and moved one code sample above the fold.

We want more of that work. The engineers who pick our SDK ask a colleague in some Slack we cannot see, then install whatever the colleague names. Our competitor pays someone to sit in those channels and answer questions on a Thursday night. We send a pricing page.

The two advocates will spend most of their time on the docs and the sample apps. The rest goes to conference talks, which I expect to produce very little in the first year. I am funding the talks because our name has to reach engineers before they need us, and I have found no cheaper way to do that.

The budget covers salaries and the booth we skipped last year. It comes out of paid acquisition, where our cost per signup climbed 60 percent since January.

At the end of Q2 I will report two numbers: time to first API call, and the share of new accounts that ship something within a week. If neither moves, cut the program.
