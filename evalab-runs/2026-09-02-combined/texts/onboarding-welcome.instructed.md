# Welcome

You made it. Your laptop is on the desk, your accounts should already work, and if they don't, message #it-help and someone will unstick you within the hour.

Here's what the first week actually looks like. Today is setup: clone the monorepo, get the test suite running locally, and meet your onboarding buddy. Tomorrow you'll pair with someone on your team and watch them work. By Thursday or Friday, most people have shipped a small change to production — a copy fix, a log line, a config tweak. That's deliberate. We'd rather you learn the deploy path on something harmless than on something that matters.

Nobody expects you to be productive this month. The codebase is fifteen years old in places, and there are decisions in it that made sense at the time and don't anymore. Ask about them. If someone can't explain why a thing works the way it does, that's useful information for both of you.

A few things worth knowing early. We write design docs before large changes, and they get comments, sometimes blunt ones. Code review is required and usually fast. On-call starts after your third month, never before. Meetings are declinable; if you don't know why you're in one, ask the organizer.

Your buddy's name and calendar link are in the onboarding email, along with the reading list — start with the architecture overview and the incident postmortems from the last year. The postmortems will teach you more about how this place runs than anything else you'll read.

Take your time. Ask questions in public channels so the answers help the next person.
