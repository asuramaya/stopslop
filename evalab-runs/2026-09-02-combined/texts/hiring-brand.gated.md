Engineering culture

We ship small.

Most changes go out the same week they're written, and if your pull request runs 900 lines someone will ask — kindly — what the first 200 could have been on their own. Code review is not a formality here. Every change gets read by a person who doesn't share your assumptions about the service, and that person is expected to say so when something reads wrong. Disagreements go in the thread, not the hallway. We'd rather argue in writing where the next engineer can find it two years later.

You'll own the design doc, the migration, and the pager. That last part is real. Engineers here are on call for what they build, which keeps everyone honest about retries, timeouts, and the 3 a.m. cost of a clever abstraction. On-call runs [X] days a quarter, staffed so it's boring most weeks, and on the weeks it isn't you won't be the only one awake.

We write a lot. Design docs go up before the big work starts, postmortems go up after incidents, and there's no blame in either. When something breaks we want the mechanism, not a name.

What you won't find is heroics as a habit, planning theater, or a rewrite of a working system because it's five years old and someone read a blog post.

Most of our engineers came from somewhere they were not trusted to make decisions. We hire people who've been burned by that and want the opposite: a small team, a clear problem, and the room to be wrong in public and fix it fast.
