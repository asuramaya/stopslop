One year ago we closed the office. Here is what we found.

Our standup, moved to a shared document, saved four hours a week per engineer. Nobody misses the room. But we lost the thing that used to happen after standup: two people lingering by the whiteboard, arguing about a schema until one of them changed their mind. We tried to rebuild that with an open video channel and got silence. What worked was pairing sessions on the calendar, ninety minutes, two names, a specific question to answer. Rahim and Elena rewrote our billing reconciliation that way in three afternoons.

Writing got harder and better. When you can't lean over and ask, you write the design doc. Our doc count tripled. So did the length of the comment threads on them, and we spent six weeks in the spring arguing about whether that was progress. We decided it was, then capped review threads at five days.

Hiring changed more than we expected. We now interview people in Lagos and Lisbon and we stopped pretending our salary bands were geography-neutral when they never had been. That conversation cost us two engineers who felt underpaid and we deserved to lose them.

The failure mode we didn't see coming: new hires drifting for their first month. Onboarding assumed osmosis. We replaced it with a named buddy, a first-week shipping task, and a Friday call where the new person explains the codebase back to us and we correct them.

We are keeping remote. We are also flying everyone to one place twice a year, because four days together buys us six months of goodwill on Slack.
