We started pairing junior engineers with senior ones last quarter, and the first version of it failed in a way I should have predicted.

The failure was scheduling. We matched eight pairs, told them to meet "regularly," and by week five three pairs had stopped entirely. Nobody was avoiding the program. They just had sprint work, and a meeting with no agenda is the first thing to fall off a calendar.

The second version fixed two things. Meetings became a standing 45 minutes on the same day every two weeks, booked by us rather than by the pair. And every mentor got a starting question to bring: what's the thing you spent more than an hour stuck on since we last talked? That question does most of the work. It gives the mentee something concrete to open with, and it surfaces the gaps that don't show up in code review, like not knowing which Slack channel to ask in or how our on-call rotation actually decides severity.

Six months in, the pairs that stuck have produced things I can point to. Two mentees now run their own design reviews. One mentor rewrote our onboarding doc because he got tired of explaining the same deploy step four times.

What I'd tell anyone setting this up: keep it smaller than you want to. We started with eight pairs across a team of thirty and should have started with four. Matching is slow if you do it well, and doing it badly is worse than not doing it. Also, ask mentees at month three whether they want to switch mentors. Two did, and both pairs got better afterward.
