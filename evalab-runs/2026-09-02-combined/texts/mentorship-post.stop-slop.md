We started pairing senior and junior engineers in March because two people quit in the same quarter and both said the same thing on their way out: they had nobody to ask.

The first version failed. I matched eight pairs by skill overlap, told them to meet monthly, and waited. Four pairs never met. Three met once. One pair, Priya and Dev, met every week and shipped a rewrite of the billing service together.

I asked Priya what she was doing that the others weren't. She said Dev brought a specific problem to every session and she brought her screen. The other pairs had booked "mentorship" with no agenda.

I rebuilt the program around the artifact. Each pair picks one thing the mentee owns: a service, or a test suite everyone has given up on. The mentor reviews that thing and nothing else. Sessions run 45 minutes, every other week, and the mentee writes the agenda 24 hours ahead. No agenda, no session, and the mentor cancels it. That rule felt harsh when I wrote it. It kills about a fifth of the sessions and it saved the rest.

Cost: two hours a month per senior engineer. I stopped asking them to volunteer and put it in their promotion criteria instead, which is why six of eight said yes.

Nine months in, the two engineers I worried about most have both shipped work I would have handed to someone two levels above them. I can't prove the program did that. I picked those two on purpose, and I would have bet on them anyway.
