Six months ago two of our juniors quit within a month of each other. Both exit conversations said a version of the same thing: nobody was watching them grow. So we started pairing people. I want to write down what worked, because most of what I read about mentorship programs describes the version that gets announced and then dies in a quarter.

The first thing we got wrong was matching by technology. We put the Postgres person with the person who wanted to learn Postgres, and both of them showed up to a meeting with nothing to say. Matching by stage worked instead. Someone two years ahead, not eight. The gap has to be small enough that the mentor still remembers being stuck on the same thing. Cadence was the other early fix. Weekly, thirty minutes, on the calendar, and the mentor holds it. Monthly ones got skipped. An hour felt like it needed an agenda, and neither person ever wrote one.

The third rule is the one managers push back on.

We told mentors not to report anything upward. No summary, no "how are they doing." The moment a mentor is a sensor, the mentee stops bringing the real questions. That one cost me two arguments with my own director.

Nine pairs are running now.

Two dissolved, which I think is fine, and one mentee asked to switch, so we switched them. What I can measure is that PR review comments from the mentees got longer and more skeptical. They argue with senior engineers now. That is what I wanted.
