# Welcome

You're on the payroll. Someone already ordered your laptop, and it's on the desk by the window with a sticky note that has your temporary password on it.

Start with the setup script in the onboarding repo. It installs the toolchain, pulls the main services, and seeds a local database with fake customer records. It takes about forty minutes and fails on step nine for roughly half of new hires. When it fails, post the error in #eng-onboarding. Someone will answer within the hour, because we all hit step nine.

Your first pull request should go up this week. We don't care if it's a typo fix in a log message. The point is to run the pipeline once while the stakes are low, so you learn where the review bot lives and how long CI takes.

Ask your onboarding buddy anything. That's the whole job description for the role. They blocked out time for you and they'd rather answer a question twice than watch you lose a day to a config file.

A few things nobody writes down. Deploys happen Tuesday and Thursday afternoons. The staging environment lies about email delivery. Marcus knows why the billing service has two caches, and he will tell you at length if you ask.

You won't understand the architecture for a couple of months. Nobody does at first, and the people who claim otherwise are reading the diagrams instead of the code.

Grab coffee from the third floor. The machine on two is broken and the facilities ticket is nine months old.
