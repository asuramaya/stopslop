## About us

We build scheduling software for outpatient medical clinics — practices with two to fifty providers, where one person at the front desk is usually holding the whole calendar together.

The company started in 2016, after our founder spent eleven months doing implementation work for a regional orthopedics group. The clinic had bought a scheduling module bundled with its EHR. Nobody used it. Staff kept a paper book and typed appointments in afterward, because the software assumed every visit was thirty minutes and every provider worked one room. Real clinics don't run that way. A post-op follow-up takes twelve minutes, a new-patient consult takes forty-five, and the same physician might be splitting Tuesday between two locations.

So we wrote something that starts from the constraints clinics actually have: variable visit lengths, room and equipment dependencies, provider templates that change week to week, and a waitlist that fills cancellations without someone phoning down a list.

Today about 1,400 clinics run their day on our scheduler. Median time from contract to first live appointment is nine days. We integrate with Epic, athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, and NextGen, and we publish our uptime at status.[company].com rather than describing it in adjectives.

We're 62 people, headquartered in Madison with staff across eleven states. Roughly a third of the team has worked in a clinic — as a medical assistant, a scheduler, a practice manager — and that shows up in what we choose to build.

We are privately held and take no revenue from advertising, data licensing, or referral fees. Clinics pay us a subscription. That is the entire business model.
