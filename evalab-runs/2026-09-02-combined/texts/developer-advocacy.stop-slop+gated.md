We hired two developer advocates in January. Two more start in June.

I made that call after reading a support ticket from a backend engineer in Rotterdam. She spent nine days wiring our webhook API into a freight system. Six of those days went to a retry behavior we describe in one sentence, on a page nothing links to. She got it working, then wrote us four hundred words about where she lost the thread. We fixed the docs that week, and she has since sent three former colleagues our way. Our sales team reaches maybe forty engineers a month. A good integration guide reaches four thousand and keeps reaching them at 2am on a Saturday, when the engineer in Rotterdam is the one debugging. She moved more pipeline in nine frustrated days than a quarter of outbound email did, as a side effect of trying to finish her own project.

We are paying people to do what she did for free.

The team owns two numbers. The first is how long a new developer takes to make a working API call, measured from signup. Ours sits at 51 minutes and we want it under ten. The second is how many questions in our forum go 48 hours without an answer from us. That number was 90 in December. Neither one tracks impressions or conference talks.

Our advocates will write code, answer questions with their own names attached, and tell our engineers when the API is bad. Cynthia starts Monday. Send her your worst integration story.
