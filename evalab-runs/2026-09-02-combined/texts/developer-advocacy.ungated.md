**Why we're funding developer relations this year**

Last quarter I read every support ticket filed by a customer in their first week with our API. The pattern was boring, and expensive.

People weren't stuck on the hard parts. They were stuck on auth. On a code sample that quietly assumed a version we stopped shipping in [March]. On a 400 that came back with no body, so you had to guess which of your four headers was wrong. Median time to first successful call was [11 minutes] for people who got there — but [38%] never did, and we mostly never heard from them, because nobody files a ticket about a product they've already closed the tab on.

That's not a docs problem, exactly. It's not an engineering problem either. It lives in the gap between them, and the gap is what developer relations is for.

So we're hiring [two] people into it. Not to run a booth. The job is to be the first user of everything we ship, before customers are: build the integration, hit the wall, then have enough standing internally to get the wall moved. That last part is why this reports into product and not marketing. A DevRel team that can only write about the product will end up writing apologies for it.

We'll know it worked if that [38%] drops and our forums answer questions faster than we do. Ask me in [six months].
