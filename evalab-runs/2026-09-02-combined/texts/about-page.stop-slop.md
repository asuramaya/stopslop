Meridian builds scheduling software for medical clinics.

Priya Raman ran the front desk at a pediatric practice in Akron for four years. She rebooked the same families over and over because she could not see, inside the practice's software, which visits a nurse practitioner was cleared to take. She kept a paper waitlist in a spiral notebook next to the phone. In 2016 she and Tomas Belido, who had written billing integrations at a hospital group, started Meridian to give her old job a better tool.

About 900 clinics use us now, most of them between two and fifteen providers. When a patient cancels a 9:40 physical, the scheduler fills the slot from the waitlist in two clicks and the patient gets a text. Providers set their own rules about visit types, room requirements, and buffer time, and the scheduler sees those rules while booking instead of learning them by getting it wrong.

We do a few things on purpose. We charge per clinic, not per seat, so nobody at the front desk shares a login. We publish our uptime and our support response times where you can read them without asking a salesperson. We will tell you when Meridian is a poor fit, which happens with large hospital systems that need deep integration with an enterprise record.

Our team is 34 people across Cleveland and Lisbon. Eleven of them worked in clinics before they worked here, and they answer support tickets alongside the engineers who wrote the code being complained about.
