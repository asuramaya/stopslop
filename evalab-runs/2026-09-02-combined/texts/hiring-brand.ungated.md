## How we work

Small changes, shipped often. A typical pull request here is under 300 lines and goes to production the same week it's written; we'd rather correct course twice than plan for a quarter and be wrong.

Every change gets read by another engineer before it merges. Reviews are for catching real problems — not for arguing about where the braces go, which is what the formatter is for. If a review thread hits ten comments, someone picks up the phone.

We're a team of [24] engineers across [four] product areas, and we've kept it deliberately flat. There are no tech leads who don't write code. The person closest to the problem makes the call, and "I was wrong about this" is a normal thing to say in a design doc.

On-call is one week in [eight], and it's quiet, because we treat a 3 a.m. page as a bug in the system rather than a fact of life. Every incident gets a write-up. Nobody gets blamed in it.

Some things we're honest about: our test suite takes [eleven minutes] and we hate it. There's a service written in [2019] that nobody enjoys touching. We're paying both down, slowly, in between the work that pays the bills.

What we look for is less about a specific stack than about how you think when the answer isn't obvious. If you like reading other people's code, you'll do well here.
