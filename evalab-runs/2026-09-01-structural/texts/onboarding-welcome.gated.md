# Welcome

Today you don't have to build anything.

Get your laptop running, find the coffee (second floor, the good machine is by the window), and meet the six people who sit nearest you. That's the whole assignment. Setup instructions are in `handbook/setup.md`; if a step is wrong, fix the step and open a PR. That's your first commit, and it counts.

A few things nobody tells you until week three.

Ask early. If you're stuck for twenty minutes, say so in your team channel. Nobody is keeping score, and the person who answers you was asking the same thing eight months ago.

Deploys are boring. We ship to production about thirty times a day. You'll do yours this week, probably a one-line change, and someone will sit with you for it. If you break something, we roll back in under two minutes and then write down what happened. Nobody gets yelled at for an outage. People do get asked what they learned.

Read the code before you judge it. Some of it is ten years old and looks strange. Usually there's a reason, and the reason is in the git blame.

Your manager will find you at 2pm. Before that, wander. Ask someone what they're working on and let them talk too long about it.

Welcome aboard. We're glad you took the offer.

Swap these for your real numbers before it ships: the deploy rate, the rollback time, the coffee location, and the 2pm manager check-in. The specifics are what make it read as yours rather than as a template — wrong ones will read worse than none.
