**About Kestrel**

Kestrel started in 2018, after eighteen months of sitting behind front desks and watching what actually happens there.

What we saw: a receptionist at a three-provider family practice in Dayton keeping a paper list of patients who wanted earlier appointments, because her scheduling system had no way to record it. When a Tuesday slot opened, she called down the list by hand. That list was the most valuable thing in the office, and no software knew it existed.

So we built scheduling software around how clinics really run. Kestrel handles the waitlist calls, the standing appointments that shift when a provider changes their Thursday, the fifteen-minute follow-up that has to land within six weeks of the procedure. It knows that a no-show at 8 a.m. costs more than one at 4 p.m., and it books accordingly.

Today about 1,400 clinics use us, most of them independent practices with two to twelve providers. We are deliberately not built for hospital systems. Those places have different problems and plenty of vendors already.

We are 31 people, based in Pittsburgh, and profitable. That last part matters more than it sounds: it means we answer to the clinics paying us rather than to a plan for what we should become in three years. Our support line is answered by people who can change your settings while you are on the phone with them.

If your schedule is running you instead of the other way around, we would like to hear about it.
