Why we're hiring a developer relations team

We shipped an API in March. Good docs, generous free tier, a launch post that did fine on Hacker News. Then we read the funnel. 4,100 signups, 380 people who made a second call, 41 who were still there in July.

The gap wasn't the product.

We went through the support tickets. People got stuck on the same four things: auth token refresh, pagination, our odd timeout default, and what to do when a webhook fires twice. Every one of those was answerable in two sentences by someone on our team. None of them was answerable by our docs at 11pm on a Saturday. That's the job we're hiring for. Not conference talks, though there will be some of that. We want someone whose full-time work is to sit where developers get stuck and clear the obstacle, whether the fix is a doc rewrite, a sample repo, a Discord answer, or a bug filed against us with the receipts attached.

Two commitments on our side, because this role dies without them. The team reports into product, not marketing, and their metric is activation, not impressions. They also get to file P1s. If they say the SDK's error messages are useless, that goes in the sprint.

The companies developers actually like are the ones that staffed this early, before they needed it to repair a reputation. We'd rather be early.
