**What I learned starting a mentorship program**

We had eleven engineers and no way for the four newest to learn anything that didn't arrive as a PR comment. So in March I asked the seniors if they'd take a mentee. Three said yes. One said no, honestly, because they were drowning — and that turned out to be the most useful answer I got, since it set the expectation that this was opt-in.

The first version failed. I paired people by tech stack, gave them a shared doc, and told them to meet "regularly." Two pairs met once. The doc stayed empty. What fixed it was making the meeting concrete: 45 minutes, every other Tuesday, on the mentee's calendar, with an agenda the mentee writes. Not the mentor. That one inversion did more than anything else, because it put the work of showing up on the person who wanted something.

The next thing we changed was the name. We stopped calling it career development, and mentees started bringing real problems — a migration they were scared of, a design review that went badly, a promo packet due in three weeks. The abstract sessions about "growth" had died within a month. Cost is about three hours a month per mentor. I told the seniors it would count on their promo packets, then made sure it actually did, which meant writing it into two review docs myself.

Six months in, four pairs are still meeting. Two dissolved, and that's fine. My advice if you want to start one is to pick three volunteers, not a program.
