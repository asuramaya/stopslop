For a small team, start with scheduled batches. They demand less operational overhead and handle most non-urgent work cleanly. Write your batch jobs to be idempotent—safe to replay if something breaks—and use straightforward logging to track what ran. When you need real-time processing (payment confirmations, user notifications), add a queue. Use a managed service like AWS SQS or Google Cloud Tasks rather than running your own Redis or RabbitMQ. This avoids the operational burden of keeping infrastructure alive and handling queue failures yourself. Combine batches for bulk work (daily reports, data cleanup) with queues for user-facing work (password resets, order confirmations). Your team stays focused on product work instead of queue operations. Scale this approach as your team grows—it still works at 10 people. Only when you hit serious throughput or latency demands should you invest in dedicated queue infrastructure and operational expertise.
