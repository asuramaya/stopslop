The clock was never `time.time()` — `__init__` defaults to `time.monotonic`, which is the drift-free choice, immune to NTP steps and settimeofday. So the named cause isn't in this file. What I found instead, in the same three lines:

**`_refill` stalled on any backwards clock step.** The old `if elapsed > 0:` guard skipped the update when `now < self._updated`, leaving the mark stranded in the future. Every later refill then also saw `elapsed <= 0` and did nothing, so the bucket issued no tokens until real time climbed back past the stale mark. A 5-second backward step froze the limiter for 5 seconds. It now takes `now` as the new mark and forfeits only the unmeasurable interval spanning the jump: after a 5s step back, one second of real time yields 1.00 tokens instead of 0.00.

This only bites a caller who injects `clock=time.time`, which is exactly what someone chasing wall-clock drift is likely to have done. With the `time.monotonic` default it is unreachable.

**What I tried and backed out.** `_refill` also discards refill credit when `elapsed * rate` is smaller than the ulp of `_tokens` — the addition rounds away, but the mark still advances to `now`. I rewrote it to advance the mark only by the time that became tokens. That was wrong: an addition below the ulp rounds to nearest, so it sometimes rounds *up*, crediting a full ulp for a sliver of time. The rewrite issued 170 tokens where 100 were earned. Under-issuing is the safe failure for a rate limiter; over-issuing is not, so the straightforward `min()` form stands. Reaching that path needs a balance-to-increment ratio past ~1e16 (a 1e17 bucket taking 0.1-token increments); at any sane capacity it cannot occur, and fixing it properly means integer tokens rather than floats, not a patch to this method.

Verification: 100,000 polls across a simulated second at 10 tokens/sec produce exactly 10.0 — no accumulated loss under hammering, no over-issue. The earlier behaviour is unchanged: `try_acquire(5)` on a drained 5-token bucket → `True` then `False`, `time_until(2)` → 2.0, `acquire(1, timeout=2)` returns True after 0.25s, `acquire(2, timeout=0.10)` returns False after 0.10s.

If you are seeing real drift in production, the remaining suspects are outside `_refill`: a caller constructing `TokenBucket(..., clock=time.time)`, or `acquire`'s wait, which goes through `Condition.wait` on real time regardless of the injected clock and wakes late under CPU contention.
