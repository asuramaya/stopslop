## How we work

Small changes, merged fast. Most pull requests here run under 200 lines and land the same day they're opened. We'd rather be wrong in an afternoon than right in a quarter.

Every engineer deploys. There's no release team and no ticket you file to get your code in front of users. You press the button, you watch the graphs, and if it breaks you're the one who rolls it back. The responsibility runs both ways, since it also means nobody can block you. We write things down. Design decisions live in a doc with a name attached, and the doc says what we chose and what we gave up. Six months later, when someone asks why the scheduler works the way it does, the answer is findable instead of folklore.

Code review is a conversation between peers, not a gate. We argue. Sometimes for a while. The person who owns the change makes the final call, and then we move.

On-call is one week in six, with a hard rule attached. If a page wakes you up, you spend the next day fixing whatever paged you. Toil nobody is allowed to remove is a bug in the team, not in the system.

We hire people who are still curious after ten years of this. Some of us came here from physics, from teaching, from professional kitchens. Nearly all of us maintain something on the side that nobody asked for.

No heroics, no crunch, and no pretending a deadline was a plan.
