Nobody schedules a sprint for the thing that's actually slowing them down.

This month's theme is technical debt, a phrase I've come to distrust. Debt implies a lender, a rate, a due date — three things our systems politely decline to provide. What we actually have is a pile of decisions made by people who were right at the time, under constraints that have since evaporated, and no record of which was which.

That last part is the real problem. The 2019 auth shim isn't bad code. It's undocumented code, and the one person who could explain it left in March.

So these pieces aren't about paying anything down. Priya walks through the migration off our custom job runner, including the two weeks lost to a queue-ordering assumption nobody knew existed. Sam argues for deleting instead of refactoring, with numbers from the checkout service: 4,000 lines gone, zero incidents since. There's also a short piece on writing the comment that explains *why* rather than *what*.

Read the deletion one first. It's the shortest, and it will annoy the most people.
