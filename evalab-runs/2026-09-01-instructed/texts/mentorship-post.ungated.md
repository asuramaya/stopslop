**We started a mentorship program. The first version was useless.**

Six pairs, monthly 1:1s, a shared doc for "goals." By week seven, four of the six pairs had quietly stopped scheduling. Nobody was mad about it. There was just nothing to say that a normal 1:1 didn't already cover.

We'd matched people on seniority and left the agenda blank. Two engineers stare at a doc titled "Career Growth" and the meeting dies.

Second version, we changed one thing: every pairing starts with a piece of work. Not a topic. An actual thing. Nadia wanted to get better at reviewing code she hadn't written, so she and her mentor picked a service neither of them owned and worked through its open PRs together for a month. Someone else wanted to run an incident. He shadowed two, then ran the third with his mentor on mute in the call.

That's the whole change. Sessions have a subject now, so people show up.

Three things I'd pass on if you're setting one up.

Six weeks, not "ongoing." Ongoing means never ending and never evaluated. At six weeks you re-up or you don't, and walking away isn't a failure.

Let people pick their own mentor. Our assignments were worse than their choices, every single time.

And put it on the calendar as work. If it's the first thing cut when the sprint slips, you've told everyone exactly what it's worth.
