Welcome to your first day

You will spend most of today setting things up and meeting people, and that's the whole plan. Nobody expects code from you this week.

Your laptop is on your desk. The setup guide in the `onboarding` repo walks through access, keys, and the local dev environment. It takes about three hours if nothing breaks, and something usually breaks around the database seeding step. When it does, ask in #eng-help. People answer there within ten minutes during working hours, and asking early is the norm here, not a sign you're struggling.

Your onboarding buddy will find you before lunch. They're an engineer outside your team who has been here at least a year, and their job is to answer the questions that feel too small to ask your manager. Use them for that. Nothing is too small.

A few things about how we work. Code review is required on every change, including one-line fixes. Reviews are about the code, and we say so directly rather than softening it into vagueness. Deploys happen several times a day; if you break production, we roll back, write up what happened, and blame the system that let it through rather than the person who pushed it. That last part is real, and you'll see it tested within your first month.

Your first task is already assigned. It's small on purpose: a real bug, in a real service, that someone would fix anyway. The point is to walk the full path from ticket to production once, while everything is still unfamiliar enough that you notice where the path is confusing. Write down what confused you. We'll fix it before the next person arrives.
