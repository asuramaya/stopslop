# About

We build scheduling software for medical clinics. That means the front-desk software that decides who gets seen at 9:40 on a Tuesday, how long the room stays occupied, and what happens when the patient before them arrives twenty minutes late.

The company started in 2016 after our founder spent a year working reception at a three-physician family practice in Portland. The clinic ran on a wall calendar and a paper waitlist, and had tried two scheduling products before giving up on both. Neither could express the rule that Dr. Ahn does not take new pediatric patients on the same day she runs the anticoagulation clinic. That rule, and about forty others like it, is the actual problem.

Today around 900 clinics use Cadence, most of them independent practices with two to fifteen providers. We are 34 people, based in Portland and Chicago, with eight of us in support. Support is staffed by people who have worked in clinics, and it answers the phone during business hours in both time zones.

Some things we have decided not to do. We do not sell patient data, and our terms say so in language a lawyer did not have to soften. We do not charge per appointment, because that penalizes clinics for being busy. We do not offer a "free" tier that quietly caps your provider count halfway through onboarding.

Scheduling is not glamorous work. Done badly it produces empty rooms, angry waiting areas, and staff who stay an hour past close. We would rather it went the other way.
