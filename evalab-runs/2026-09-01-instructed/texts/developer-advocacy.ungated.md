# Why we're hiring two people for developer relations

We shipped the API eighteen months ago and told ourselves the docs would carry it. They didn't.

Look at last quarter's support queue. Roughly half the tickets weren't bugs at all — they were people stuck at step three of a quickstart that quietly assumed you'd already read a page we never linked to. One customer burned most of a day on an auth error that one sentence would have prevented. We eventually wrote the sentence. Tickets mentioning that error fell about 80% the following month.

That's the argument. The gap between "the product can do this" and "someone got it working on a Tuesday afternoon" is real work, and nobody here owns it. It's writing. It's answering the same question in four places until the answer lands somewhere findable. It's sitting in a Discord thread at 11pm while a stranger pastes a stack trace.

So: two hires, starting in March. One focused on docs and sample code, one on the community side.

A caveat about what this isn't. Developer relations here is not marketing with a hoodie on, and we're not going to measure it in impressions or conference talks given. The number we care about is time-to-first-successful-call — how long from signup to a request that returns 200. It's eleven minutes for a lucky developer and never for too many others.

We'd rather fix that than write another launch post about how easy we are to integrate.
