## Engineering culture

Most new engineers ship to production in their first week. Deploys take ninety seconds and roll back just as fast, so there is nothing precious about the first one.

We are small. You will own things end to end: the design doc, the code, the 3am page when it breaks. That is the honest version of the job. Engineers who write code and hand it over never find out whether their ideas were any good.

Code review here is slow on purpose. We would rather spend an hour arguing about an interface than six months living with the wrong one. Reviews are blunt and they are about the code. If someone tells you your abstraction is wrong, that is the respect, not the opposite of it.

We write things down. Every non-obvious decision gets a design doc listing the options we rejected and why. It is tedious. It is also why someone who joined last month can read three files and understand why the scheduler works the way it does.

No hero culture. If a launch needs a weekend, we planned it badly, and we say that in the retro instead of buying pizza.

You get real time for work with no ticket attached: the refactor you keep flinching at, the script that saves everyone twenty minutes a day. About one day in ten. Nobody tracks it.

We hire people who argue well and change their minds.
