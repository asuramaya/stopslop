# Why we're funding devrel

We shipped an SDK last March and watched the support queue fill with questions the docs technically answered. Technically. People were finding the right page and still not getting unblocked, because the page told them what a method did without telling them why they'd want it.

That gap is what developer relations is for, and it's why we've hired three people into it this quarter.

The case is boring and financial. Our median enterprise deal takes 71 days from first contact to signature, and 40 of those days are a developer on the customer's side trying to get a prototype running. Every week we cut off that middle stretch is a week of pipeline we get back. Two of our largest accounts last year started as someone building a weekend project against our free tier, hitting a wall, and getting an actual answer in our Discord within the hour.

We're also tired of learning about our own rough edges from churn interviews. Devrel sits closer to the people using the thing than sales or support does, early enough that we can fix the API rather than write a workaround into the FAQ.

What this looks like in practice: rewritten quickstarts with runnable code, someone answering in the community forum during business hours in two time zones, and conference talks only where our users already are. No mascot, no swag budget to speak of.

We'll know it worked if time-to-first-successful-call drops below fifteen minutes and support ticket volume per active developer falls. If those numbers don't move by Q2, we'll say so.
