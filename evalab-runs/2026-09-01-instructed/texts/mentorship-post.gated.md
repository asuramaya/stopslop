# We started a mentorship program and it almost didn't survive month two

The first version was a disaster. I paired eight juniors with eight seniors, sent a calendar invite titled "Mentorship 1:1," and considered the thing launched. Six weeks later, four of the pairs had quietly stopped meeting. Nobody told me. I found out from a retro comment.

The problem wasn't commitment. It was that neither person knew what the meeting was for. A senior engineer with no agenda defaults to "so, how's it going?" and a junior with no agenda says "good." Thirty minutes of that, twice, and the invite starts looking optional.

So we rebuilt it around artifacts. Every pair now picks one thing the mentee is actually stuck on — a design doc they can't start, a review they keep getting torn apart in, a system they've never traced end to end — and the sessions exist to move that one thing. When it's done, they pick another or they stop. Ending is a legitimate outcome.

Two other rules that mattered. Mentors get their meeting time counted against project load, in writing, by their manager. And we cap pairings at three months, because an indefinite commitment is one nobody feels able to exit.

Nine months in, eleven of fourteen pairs are still running past their first renewal. The three that ended, ended cleanly.

If you're setting one up, don't start with matching. Start with the question of what a mentee brings to the table on a Tuesday afternoon when they'd rather be closing tickets.
