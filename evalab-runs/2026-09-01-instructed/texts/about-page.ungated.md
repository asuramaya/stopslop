## About Kestrel

We started Kestrel in 2019 after watching a front-desk coordinator at a Portland dermatology practice keep three browser tabs, a paper appointment book, and a hand-drawn sticky-note grid open at once. She had built the grid herself. Her scheduling software could not tell her which of the four exam rooms had the laser in it.

That is the gap we work in. Not electronic health records, which plenty of companies already do, but the twenty minutes before a patient walks in, when someone has to reconcile a provider's block schedule against room availability, equipment, and a cancellation that came in at 6 a.m.

Kestrel schedules 340 clinics across 19 states, mostly independent practices with two to fifteen providers. We handle the things that break: double-booked rooms, providers who split time across sites, recurring physical-therapy series, prior authorizations that expire mid-course. Our waitlist filled 71% of same-day cancellations across our customer base last quarter.

We are 34 people. About a third came out of clinic operations rather than software, which is why our onboarding calls sound less like demos and more like someone asking how you actually run a Tuesday.

Two commitments. We do not sell patient data, to anyone, for any purpose. And we do not charge per-provider fees that punish you for hiring; pricing is flat by clinic size, and it does not change when you add a nurse practitioner.

If your current system has your staff inventing sticky notes, call us.
