# Welcome

You're here. Someone will hand you a laptop within the hour, and by Thursday you'll have shipped something to production. That isn't a stretch goal. It's the point of your first week.

Start with the repo called `handbook`. Clone it, read `getting-started.md`, and follow it exactly. When a step is wrong — a step will be wrong — fix it and open a PR. Your first merged change should be that fix. It keeps the docs honest, and it puts your name in the log on day one.

**Things nobody tells you until week three:**

- Deploys go out about forty times a day. Yours will too. If you break something, roll back first and explain after. Nobody has ever gotten a talking-to for a rollback.
- The `#eng-help` channel is faster than reading source. Ask there before you spend two hours guessing.
- Every PR needs one reviewer. Not two, not a committee. Pick whoever touched the file last; `git log` will tell you who that is.
- Meetings are optional unless you own an agenda item. Decline them. Really.

Your manager will find you before lunch. Bring questions about anything: the roadmap, why the billing service is written in Go, how salary bands work. All of it is fair game today, and still fair game in March.

What we ask back is small. Write things down. Say out loud when you're stuck. Leave the code a little clearer than you found it, which is the whole culture doc as far as we're concerned.

Coffee is on the second floor. The good machine is the ugly one.
