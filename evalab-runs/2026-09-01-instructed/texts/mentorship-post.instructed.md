# Starting a mentorship program on our team

We started pairing engineers in April. Six months in, here's what worked and what I'd do differently.

The original plan was ambitious: monthly goal-setting docs, a skills matrix, quarterly reviews of mentee progress. We shipped none of it. What survived was a standing 45-minute biweekly slot and a shared doc where the mentee writes what they want to talk about before the meeting. That last part matters more than it sounds. Without an agenda, the meeting becomes a status update, and the mentee already gives status updates to their manager.

Matching was the hardest call. I tried skill-adjacency first, putting a backend engineer wanting frontend depth with our strongest frontend person. Two of those four pairs fizzled by month two. The pairs that lasted were matched on the kind of problem the mentee was stuck on, not the technology. Someone who couldn't get their designs through review needed a mentor who was good at getting designs through review, regardless of stack.

Three things I got wrong. I didn't set an end date, so nobody knew when it was okay to stop, and two pairs limped along past usefulness. I asked mentors to volunteer without adjusting their project load, which meant the most in-demand people were also the most overcommitted. And I never told mentors what the program was for, so each one invented a purpose.

Nine of eleven pairs are still meeting. The two that stopped had good reasons. I'd call that fine, not a success.
